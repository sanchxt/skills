# Analytics and iteration

Use when the user supplies performance data, asks why posts underperformed, or wants a growth/testing plan.

## Do not diagnose 3–4 posts from views alone

Low early reach is evidence that the account has not yet found a strong audience-content match; it does not identify the cause by itself. Ask for or use platform-native stop, retention, action, and conversion metrics. Treat the first batch of posts as learning data, not a final verdict on either format.

## Analyze four layers

| Layer | Useful signals | Likely issue when weak |
|---|---|---|
| Stop | YouTube stayed-to-watch; earliest retention; first-frame response | subject framing, first visual, text/line clarity, weak contrast |
| Stay | engaged views, AVD, APV, completion, curve shape, rewatches | excess setup, broken story/visual progression, delayed proof/drop, illegibility |
| Act | shares/sends, likes, comments, saves, qualitative replies | weak payoff, low social value, generic question, no identity/emotion |
| Convert | profile visits, follows/subscribers per engaged viewer, returning viewers | unclear account promise, mismatched subjects, generic or premature CTA |

For music-led edits, also inspect the curve at the first cut, first text card, pre-drop, drop, and loop. For voiceover stories, inspect the end of the hook, each context line, pivot, proof, meaning, and CTA.

## Use comparable baselines

Do not invent universal retention or engagement thresholds. Compare each platform, format, subject lane, and duration band to the account’s rolling median once enough comparable posts exist. Normalize actions:

- shares/sends per reach or engaged viewer;
- likes per reach;
- comments per reach;
- profile visits per reach;
- follows/subscribers per 1,000 engaged viewers.

On YouTube, use engaged views, stayed-to-watch, AVD, and APV for meaningful Shorts comparisons; raw view counting changed in August 2026.

## Curve diagnosis

- **Immediate cliff:** weak/unclear first frame, unfamiliar subject without universal stakes, black/logo intro, or generic hook.
- **Drop after hook:** body repeats the promise or the edit has no visible direction.
- **Drop on text:** too much reading, weak contrast, bad safe-area placement, or text that explains the obvious.
- **Middle sag:** no new consequence, contrast, proof, motion, dialogue, or musical escalation.
- **Drop before payoff/drop:** tease lasted too long or pre-drop build added no value.
- **Drop during the effect:** subject became unreadable or the transition mattered more than the moment.
- **Drop during CTA:** CTA is long, premature, generic, or breaks the aesthetic.
- **Spike:** delight, a strong image, rewatchable sync, shared moment, or confusion; inspect before judging.

## Test one meaningful variable

Useful matched tests include:

- contradiction hook versus known-result/hidden-cost hook;
- iconic face versus action-first opening;
- text-free versus 3–8 word first-frame text;
- 15–25 versus 25–40 second voiceover cut from the same factual spine;
- 8–15 versus 16–25 second music-led cut using the same song and payoff;
- clean motion-match grammar versus heavier impact effects;
- voiceover-open versus authentic-dialogue-open;
- emotional reframe versus genuine interpretive-question ending.

Keep the subject, payoff, track/version, and most of the body stable when testing the hook. Do not change hook, length, song, effects, subject, and CTA and then claim one variable caused the result.

## Initial learning block

Across roughly the first 18–30 strong posts, keep one channel promise while sampling both formats. This is a practical learning block, not a statistical guarantee.

- Tag every post by format, subject lane, hook family, duration, audio architecture, effect grammar, ending, and platform.
- Avoid mechanically filling every combination; choose good subjects first.
- Review early metrics after they stabilize, then revisit after several days.
- Do not declare a post dead from an alleged universal “test batch.”
- Reuse winning principles, not the exact script, clip order, or effect template.

Suggested content pillars under one promise:

- **Story Behind the Moment:** concise, source-backed voiceover transformations and hidden context.
- **The Moment in Motion:** music-led character/person edits that express one emotion or change without narration.

## Growth and engagement system

- Keep the channel promise narrower than the subject list: the connective tissue is iconic people/characters and the moments that changed how audiences see them.
- Use repeatable series labels and recognizable caption/first-frame styling, but vary substance and edit grammar.
- Package each platform natively from a clean master. Do not compare raw view counts across platforms as equivalent.
- On Instagram, prioritize watch time and send-worthy meaning; use Trial Reels for alternate hooks or unfamiliar subjects when available.
- On TikTok, use current Creator Search Insights and comment insights when available to find audience wording, questions, and content gaps.
- On YouTube, group comparable formats/series in Analytics, build a useful library, and favor sustainable quality over an arbitrary daily quota.
- Reply to genuine early comments, use repeated questions as future-video ideas, and pin a comment that extends the conversation rather than begging for engagement.
- Make following useful: promise a recognizable next kind of story/edit. A viewer follows a series expectation, not an isolated CTA.

## Revision output

When revising from analytics, provide:

1. evidence-backed diagnosis ranked by likely impact;
2. earliest observable failure and the metric that supports it;
3. revised first-frame/hook package;
4. one body or clip-progression change;
5. one payoff/drop/CTA change when action is weak;
6. one next-post hypothesis and the metric that would falsify it.
