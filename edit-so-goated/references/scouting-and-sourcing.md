# Scouting and sourcing

Use this reference for subject discovery, current relevance, factual claims, quotations, interviews, fictional canon, release timing, or source/clip leads.

## Return candidates that can actually be edited

Unless the user requests another count, return two candidates. Vary the category or emotional mechanism when useful. A candidate may be a real person, athlete, musician, actor, director or other film/TV worker, creator, public figure, fictional character, game character, ensemble, rivalry, relationship, or one famous moment with hidden context.

For each candidate provide:

1. **Subject + one-line premise.**
2. **Why now or why evergreen.** For current interest, give dated evidence rather than saying “trending.”
3. **Format verdict.** `Story-first`, `Edit-first`, or `Works for both`.
4. **STORY score** and **EDIT score**, with the weakest dimension named.
5. **Voiceover angle.** One precise transformation/question and its payoff.
6. **Music-led angle.** Target emotion, opening image, visual contrast, and drop image.
7. **Source/clip feasibility.** What proof exists and what rights uncertainty remains.
8. **Sources.** Two to four direct, labeled links for factual candidates.

Replace a weak candidate instead of padding the list with fame.

## Two 0–10 fit scores

Score each dimension 0–2.

### STORY

- **Stakes:** a loss, pressure, conflict, rejection, failure, moral test, or identity problem.
- **Turn:** one decision, chance, game, performance, episode, person, or realization changes the direction.
- **Outcome:** the payoff is concrete and meaningfully contrasts with the opening.
- **Recognition:** the subject is known, timely, or attached to a universal conflict a cold viewer understands.
- **Yield of proof:** credible facts, scenes, interviews, dialogue, or visible evidence support the arc.

### EDIT

- **Iconicity:** the face, costume, venue, pose, moment, or silhouette reads quickly.
- **Dynamic motion:** enough usable action, camera movement, expressions, or matchable gestures exist.
- **Emotional contrast:** the footage supports a visible before/after, calm/chaos, defeat/victory, innocence/power, isolation/community, or another legible change.
- **Track/drop fit:** the subject has a clear build, switch, reveal, transformation, or impact moment that can land on a musical phrase or drop.
- **Source-rights viability:** suitable owned, licensed, permissioned, official, public-domain, or platform-remixable assets exist, or a practical safe fallback exists.

Interpretation:

- 8–10: strong fit for that format.
- 6–7: viable only with a sharper angle or better footage.
- 0–5: replace unless the user has a compelling reason or unique assets.

Scores prioritize concepts; they do not predict views.

## Real-person route

Use this evidence order:

1. direct interview, speech, memoir, verified account, official organization/league/award record, or contemporaneous primary source;
2. original reporting from a reputable publication;
3. high-quality documentary or secondary profile that identifies its evidence;
4. Wikipedia, fan pages, Reddit, reposts, and existing social videos only as discovery leads.

For a comeback or transformation establish the precise before state, real stakes, documented pivot, subject agency, visible outcome, and dates needed to avoid misleading compression.

For sensitive claims, attribute the account (“she later said…”), preserve context, distinguish allegation/opinion/diagnosis/documented event, and never invent private thoughts.

## Fictional-canon route

Establish season/episode/chapter/game chronology, what the scene visibly shows, relevant dialogue, earlier behavior, later proof, and official creator/cast commentary when useful. Label claims as:

- **Canon:** explicitly shown or stated.
- **Interpretation:** a defensible reading of the work.
- **Inference:** plausible but not explicit.
- **Fan theory:** audience speculation, labeled as such.

Do not flatten a season- or game-long arc into a false single cause. A scene may crystallize or reveal change without causing all of it.

## Current-interest route

When the user asks what is hot now, search across the requested countries/platforms and look for dated triggers:

- release, trailer, casting announcement, award, match, anniversary, reunion, tour, album, documentary, adaptation, update, DLC, remake, or rediscovered clip;
- current search interest, platform trend pages, verified official posts, and recent reputable coverage;
- fan discussion as evidence of vocabulary and debate, not as proof of facts.

State the date checked, the exact trigger, and whether momentum appears rising, sustained, or speculative. Avoid attaching a trend to an audience that does not match the channel promise.

## Claim ledger

Use a compact internal ledger before scripting:

| Claim | Best source | Exact support | Date | Confidence | Script-safe wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not script low-confidence material as fact. When an exact clip timestamp cannot be verified in the actual media, provide search terms and mark it unverified.
