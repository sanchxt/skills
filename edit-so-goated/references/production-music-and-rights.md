# Production, music, clips, and rights

Read when the user requests clips, exact timestamps, songs, effects, an edit timeline, or a publishable production plan.

## Clip source record

For every recommended interview, scene, performance, or sports moment provide:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Source | publisher/channel, exact title, direct URL, date |
| In/out | timestamp verified in the actual media, or explicitly unverified |
| Content | speaker/action and a short exact quotation when needed |
| Function | recognition, hook, pressure/build, turn, proof/drop, meaning/button |
| Label | speaker/date/episode/context to show if needed |
| Rights | licensed, official remix, public domain, permission needed, or unknown |
| Fallback | original narration, owned/stock asset, still, graphic, or another cleared source |

Do not invent timestamps or advise downloading media in violation of service terms. A public video is a viewing lead, not proof that reuse is licensed.

## First frame and captions

- Use one dominant subject, motion or expression, strong separation, and one instantly readable idea.
- Avoid black frames, slow fades, channel logos, and empty establishing shots before the premise.
- Manually correct names, dialogue, and captions; break lines by meaning.
- Emphasize a few key words rather than animating every word.
- Keep faces and text inside a conservative 9:16 safe area and verify in each platform UI.
- Export a high-resolution, clean master without another platform’s watermark.

## Music verification

For each track log:

- exact title, artist, version/remix/edit, and source;
- date checked, platform, region, and account/use type;
- available/allowed duration;
- intro, phrase, riser, silence, drop/chorus, secondary accent, and outro timestamps in that version;
- lyric or emotional conflict with the edit;
- rights status and a structurally similar safe fallback.

Music catalogs and trends are unstable. Check platform trend/library pages and the user’s actual account when needed; do not infer availability across platforms.

## Audio architecture

### Voiceover story

Keep the voice dominant. Use sparse music under factual pressure, create space immediately before the pivot, land the release on the decision or first proof, and let authentic sound or the face breathe at the payoff.

### Music-led edit

Map clips to phrases before individual beats. Establish a recognizable texture immediately, escalate motion or contrast through the build, use a brief reset when it increases impact, land the strongest visual on the main release, then hold or loop intentionally. Beat-synced cuts can increase audiovisual pleasure, but constant cutting reduces contrast.

## Cross-platform rights cautions

- Prefer licensed, permissioned, original, public-domain, official press, or in-product remix assets.
- Platform music licenses vary by platform, country, account type, content type, and duration. A song licensed inside one app does not automatically cover an exported master or another app.
- YouTube music added through the Shorts tools follows its Shorts licensing system; external use can receive standard Content ID claims or removal requests. Shorts over one minute with an active claim can be blocked. YouTube’s Audio Library is its known copyright-safe option.
- TikTok recommends its Commercial Music Library or separately cleared music for content promoting a brand, product, or service.
- Instagram’s licensed library is intended for personal, non-commercial use and may be restricted for business accounts; Meta Sound Collection is the commercial-safe alternative Meta identifies.
- Credit, short excerpts, cropping, pitch/speed changes, borders, captions, effects, or “no copyright intended” do not create permission.
- Copyright exceptions are jurisdiction- and context-specific. Do not give legal guarantees.
- A transformatively edited video may satisfy a platform’s originality/monetization review while still carrying copyright risk.

## Originality standard

Make the channel’s interpretation visible through the premise, selection, juxtaposition, timing, typography, sound design, effects, or commentary. Do not rely on minimally changed movie/TV/game/sports footage or a repeated template. Save the research and rights notes with the project.
