# Voiceover stories

Use for narrated or hybrid EditSoGoated videos.

## Select one spine

Do not summarize an entire life, career, franchise, season, or game. Select one transformation with a clear before state, pressure, pivot, visible proof, and meaning.

Use **Promise → Pressure → Pivot → Proof → Meaning → Next**:

1. **Promise:** recognition, tension, and truthful destination.
2. **Pressure:** two or three consequences that make the change difficult, not a date list.
3. **Pivot:** the decision, opportunity, relationship, performance, or realization where direction changes.
4. **Proof:** footage, action, result, dialogue, score, award, changed behavior, or callback that visibly supports the claim.
5. **Meaning:** one specific interpretation that changes how the opening image feels.
6. **Next:** optional natural action after the emotional resolution.

Connect beats through conflict and consequence—often “but,” “so,” “therefore,” or “that meant”—instead of “and then” chronology. Each line must change the situation, increase pressure, reveal proof, or deepen meaning.

## Default length system

Start with 25–40 seconds and approximately 65–100 narrated words. This is a testing default for EditSoGoated, not a platform threshold.

| Version | Typical scope | Starting word budget |
|---|---|---:|
| 15–25 sec cutdown | one pressure beat, pivot, proof | 40–65 |
| 25–40 sec default | two pressure beats, pivot, proof, meaning | 65–100 |
| 40–55 sec extended | only when a second turn or authentic clip earns it | 100–135 |

Estimate timing at the intended voice’s natural emotional pace and subtract room for authentic dialogue, pauses, and silent proof. Read or synthesize a draft when practical. Cut duplicated setup, weak adjectives, chronology, and redundant interpretation before increasing speed.

## Line-level writing

- Use concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Keep context minimal but sufficient for a cold viewer.
- Vary sentence rhythm; intentional fragments are allowed.
- Use exact dates/numbers only when they sharpen stakes or prevent a false impression.
- Do not narrate what an authentic clip already says or what the image proves.
- Preserve uncertainty and attribution from the research ledger.
- Avoid generic inspiration language that could close five unrelated stories.
- Keep citations, timestamps, and editor directions outside the pasteable block.

## Payoff and ending tests

The final line should pass at least one test:

- **Reframe:** changes the meaning of the opening image.
- **Release:** converts pressure into earned awe, relief, joy, grief, admiration, or ambiguity.
- **Callback:** repeats an early image or phrase with new meaning.
- **Conversation:** resolves the factual promise, then opens a defensible interpretation.
- **Loop:** flows naturally into the opening without undoing closure.

If the ending merely says the subject “proved everyone wrong,” find the more specific human meaning.

## Hybrid use of authentic dialogue

An interview or scene fragment can replace narration when it adds credibility, character, or emotion. Verify the source, exact words, context, and timestamp. Let the clip breathe; do not stack narration over it. Always provide a voiceover or licensed-visual fallback when the media cannot be cleared.
