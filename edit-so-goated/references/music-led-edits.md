# Music-led edits

Use when the finished video has no narrator. Dialogue or natural sound may appear, but the song, visuals, text, and edit rhythm carry the concept.

## Choose the subtype

- **Pure aesthetic:** iconic presence, movement, style, chemistry, dominance, beauty, menace, joy, or nostalgia; minimal or no text.
- **Text-led micro-story:** 1–4 short cards create setup, turn, and payoff without narration.
- **Dialogue-led:** one or two verified lines form the premise and the song/drop provides the emotional expansion.
- **Transformation edit:** visual before/after, costume or power change, rookie/legend, defeat/victory, chaos/control.
- **Parallel or relationship edit:** two people, timelines, rivals, roles, or mirrored scenes connected through match cuts.

Do not default every subject to heavy effects. Let the source footage and track decide whether the edit should be clean, velocity-driven, impact-heavy, dialogue-led, nostalgic, cinematic, or restrained.

## Build a visual micro-arc

Use **Signal → Build → Turn → Drop/Proof → Button/Loop**:

1. **Signal:** make the subject and emotional proposition legible in the first frame. Use motion, expression, a recognizable detail, or a very short text hook.
2. **Build:** 2–5 clips establish the “before,” accumulate energy, or repeat a visual motif. Each clip must progress motion, status, emotion, or anticipation.
3. **Turn:** a look, line, impact, silence, pose, costume change, camera move, or compositional match prepares the switch.
4. **Drop/Proof:** land the strongest recognizable action or transformation on the musical release. Effects amplify the subject; they do not obscure it.
5. **Button/Loop:** end on an emotional after-image, quote fragment, held expression, identity statement, or natural visual/audio return to the first frame.

For an 8–12 second edit, Signal and Build may occupy one musical phrase and the drop one phrase. For a 15–25 second edit, use two escalating phrases or a pre-drop reset. Longer edits need a second meaningful change, not merely more clips.

## Beat and phrase mapping

Mark musical structure before choosing transitions:

- first recognizable texture or lyric;
- phrase boundaries;
- kick/snare or dominant pulse;
- riser, fill, held note, breath, or silence;
- downbeat/drop/chorus/bass entrance;
- secondary accents inside the drop;
- outro or loop point.

Cutting on beats can increase audiovisual pleasure, but not every beat deserves a cut. Prioritize phrase changes, strong accents, motion completions, eyelines, and emotional turns. Use off-beat holds or silence intentionally to make the next sync point feel stronger.

## Transition and effect grammar

Select one primary grammar and one accent family. Examples:

- **Motion match:** body or camera movement continues across the cut.
- **Shape/pose match:** silhouette, face angle, prop, costume, or composition aligns.
- **Impact:** whip, zoom, shake, flash, blur, chromatic split, or frame stutter on a strong accent.
- **Velocity:** speed ramp or time-remap leads into an action or drop.
- **Texture/memory:** film burn, grain, halation, gate weave, light leak, date card.
- **Power/state shift:** color, aspect, scale, sharpen, mask reveal, or lighting changes at the turn.

Use effects to clarify hierarchy: lighter in the build, strongest at the decisive switch, cleaner after the drop when the source moment should be seen. Repeated maximal effects flatten contrast and reduce legibility.

## Clip selection

Choose clips by function rather than fame alone:

| Function | Useful footage |
|---|---|
| Recognition | iconic close-up, costume, uniform, venue, entrance, signature gesture |
| Establish state | calm, loss, isolation, weakness, innocence, chaos, rivalry |
| Accumulate | repeated action, escalating motion, alternating expressions, parallel scenes |
| Turn | glance, stance, line, first hit, reveal, costume change, crowd inhale |
| Proof/drop | victory, power reveal, performance, embrace, walk, transformation, signature moment |
| Button | held face, aftermath, walk-off, reaction, mirrored opening shot |

Favor clips with clean motion vectors, visible expressions, compatible camera direction, or meaningful contrast. Generic montage footage weakens the premise.

## Text, dialogue, and hooks

- Opening text should normally carry one idea in 3–8 words.
- Text cards should change the meaning of the next image, not describe what is obvious.
- Keep only one dominant text message at a time and place it inside platform-safe areas.
- Verify dialogue exactly; identify source and timestamp in production notes.
- A text-free edit must still communicate through recognizable imagery and visual relation.

## Song choice

Choose emotional and structural fit before popularity. A good candidate has an appropriate mood, a usable hook section, a build or contrast point, a clear release, enough licensed duration, and lyrics that do not fight the subject.

For every recommended track verify the exact version, relevant platform/region availability, account/use restrictions, usable duration, and drop/phrase timestamps. Provide a rights-safe fallback with a similar energy curve. Never call audio “trending” without current evidence.

## Default output

Deliver a concept, emotion, micro-arc, three hook packages, recommended style, 2–4 current song options, one chosen song, a track-specific timeline, clip functions/search terms, transitions/effects, text/dialogue, ending/loop, packaging, and rights notes. Keep the plan executable in an editor; avoid generic advice such as “add cool transitions.”
