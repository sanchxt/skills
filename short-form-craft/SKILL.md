# Short-form craft

A short is a chain of reasons to keep watching. The viewer decides again every second or two, with their thumb already moving. Your job is to win each of those decisions and then pay them off with something worth sending to a friend.

That's the whole game. It's what all three platforms measure, in different words:

- **YouTube Shorts:** *viewed vs. swiped away* (decided in the first 1–2 seconds), then *average % viewed*.
- **Instagram Reels:** *watch time*, *sends per reach* and *likes per reach*. Sends (DM shares) are the biggest lever for reaching non-followers.
- **All of them:** replays and real comment threads help. Reposted or lightly altered content gets suppressed.

So: **stop → stay → payoff → send.** Every choice in a script should serve one of those four. More platform detail is in [references/platforms.md](references/platforms.md), dated; refresh it with a search when it's stale.

## Idea size beats writing polish

The most common failure isn't bad writing. It's a *small idea* written nicely. A tidy, clever script about something nobody cares about still gets swiped. Before writing, check that the idea is **big** on at least one of these axes:

- **It affects the viewer:** their money, safety, phone, job, relationships, or status. ("India's biggest digital-signature company just got blocked by Chrome. Are you affected?")
- **Everyone's already talking about it,** and this video gives the take, breakdown or inside view they haven't seen.
- **It looks impressive or impossible:** a result people can't believe was made this way, or this fast.
- **High stakes or high emotion:** money on the line, someone in trouble, something taboo, a genuinely sharp take.
- **Instantly sendable:** it's so exactly "you" or "us" that people tag someone without thinking.

If an idea is merely "useful" or "relatable-ish", make it bigger before writing, or replace it. A feature tip, a mild observation or a quiet joke is filler unless it's spectacular.

**Energy:** the delivery should feel modern, confident and fast, like someone who's ahead of the curve and slightly excited to tell you. Human doesn't mean quiet or literary. Understatement is a seasoning for specific moments, not the default voice.

## How to write one (do this every time)

**1. Find the angle before writing anything.** Privately list 15+ one-line angles, fast. The first five will be what everyone else has already posted, so keep going. Keep the one to three that are *specific*, *a little surprising*, and that you'd honestly forward to someone. If nothing clears that bar, say so and offer the best you have, rather than dressing up a weak idea.

**1b. Pick the format** from [references/formats.md](references/formats.md): the container the payoff needs, different from the other ideas in the batch and from the account's recent posts, with one twist. Variety of format is part of what keeps an account from feeling machine-made.

**2. Write the payoff first.** Decide the last line, image or reveal before the hook. A hook is a promise, and you can't write a promise until you know what you're delivering. If the payoff is "a lesson" or "a summary", the idea isn't ready.

**3. Write at least five hooks, each from a different archetype** ([references/hooks.md](references/hooks.md)). Pick the one that makes the payoff feel close but not yet in reach. A hook works through what's on screen, what's said and what's written together. Plan all three, and give them different jobs.

**4. Write the script for the ear** ([references/human-voice.md](references/human-voice.md)). Each beat answers the previous question and opens a smaller new one ([references/retention.md](references/retention.md)). Start at the most interesting moment, and slip context in afterwards as half a sentence.

**5. Scroll-test, then cut** ([references/critic.md](references/critic.md)). Read it as a bored viewer, second by second. Fix the weakest moment and cut 15–20% of the words. Run the AI-tells check. Rewrite the lines that fail instead of explaining them.

**6. Deliver** in the format below.

## What does the most work

- **Specific beats general, every time.** A number, a name, an object, an exact quote or an exact price is worth ten adjectives. "₹14,000 of subscriptions" beats "lots of money". "Saved as 'do not answer'" beats "I still think about him".
- **Start late, leave early.** Begin mid-situation. End on the payoff line and stop: no recap, no moral, no "and that's why…".
- **Something changes every few seconds:** new information, a new shot, a turn or an escalation. Cut on meaning, not on a timer. A held shot after fast cuts hits hard.
- **One video, one idea.** If you need "also", it's two videos.
- **Visible beats described.** If it can be shown, show it and let the voice say something else.
- **Shorter wins ties.** Speech runs at roughly 2.5–3 words per second, so 30 seconds is about 75–90 words. Use the shortest version that still lands the payoff.
- **Earn the send.** Before finishing, answer: *who would send this to whom, and what would they type with it?* ("this is literally you", "we need to try this", "WHY is this so accurate"). If you can't answer, sharpen the payoff or the specificity.

## Output format (default)

Give complete, usable work and nothing padded. Per video:

```
### <working title> - <format>, ~<N>s

**Hooks** (pick marked ★)
1. ★ [on screen: …] SAY: "…"  TEXT: "…"
2. …  (3–5 options, each a different archetype)

**Script**
[0:00] (visual) SPEAKER: line
       TEXT: on-screen words (only when they add something)
[0:03] …
(Voiceover accounts: also the narration alone in one copy-paste block.)

**Caption:** 1–3 lines that add to the video, not a transcript. CTA only if natural.
**Cover text:** (Instagram) 2–6 words
**Note:** one line - the bet this video is making, or what to test.
```

Hashtags, keyword lists, titles for other platforms, analytics plans and research write-ups come only when asked for, or when the account skill says so. Don't append generic advice.

**Ideas mean full scripts.** Every idea delivered is a complete, filmable video, with hooks, the full script (every spoken word or skit line) and visuals, never just a premise or a few sample lines. When the user asks for "ideas" without a number, give 3 complete videos. Only when they ask for more than 5, or explicitly want a quick list, give one-line premises with hooks, fully script the top 3, and offer to script the rest.

## Integrity

Never invent things that would be presented as real: personal stories, client results, numbers, quotes, news, or "trending" sounds. For real-life stories, ask the user for what actually happened, or write it with clearly marked `[REAL DETAIL]` slots. Fiction is fine when the format is obviously fiction. Check anything time-sensitive (news, trends, songs) with a web search before calling it current.

The account skill's voice and rules override anything generic here.
