# Platform notes (last checked 2026-11-04)

These change, so search again before stating anything here as current, and update this file when you do.

## Instagram Reels
- Adam Mosseri (Jan 2025) named the top three ranking signals as **watch time**, **likes per reach** and **sends per reach**. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers. Some 2026 marketing blogs also mention replay rate and conversation signals; that's unconfirmed.
- **Originality (30 April 2026):** reuploaded content that hasn't been significantly changed is excluded from recommendations (Explore and Reels feed for non-followers). The rule was extended from Reels to photos and carousels. Memes, edits and clips must be materially altered: your own performance, edit, commentary or framing. Sources: [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator), [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/05/04/reposted-photos-and-carousels-now-face-reach-penalties-on-instagram/).
- **Trial Reels:** a reel can be shown to non-followers first. Use it to test new formats on an account without disturbing followers.
- Cover text shows on the profile grid, so give it a readable 2–6 words.

## YouTube Shorts
- The first gate is **viewed vs. swiped away**, decided in about 1–2 seconds. Then **average % viewed / average view duration** decides further distribution. YouTube publishes no official benchmarks; creator-reported "good" numbers are roughly 70%+ viewed. ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/))
- Each Short is tested on a small seed audience first. A weak result stops distribution. Volume plus consistency gives more tests.
- Titles matter less than the first frame and on-screen text, but they still help search and suggested results.
- Reused content (unedited clips, other people's material with little added) risks monetisation and reach.

## TikTok
- The same first-seconds dynamic applies. Sound and trend participation matter more than elsewhere. Velocity/"hard" edits with bass-heavy audio (phonk etc.) remain a staple edit style.
