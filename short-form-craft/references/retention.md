# Retention: structure and pacing

The hook buys you about three seconds. After that, each beat has to answer something and leave something open. If the viewer ever feels they already know how this ends, they leave.

## The engine: answer one question, open a smaller one

```
HOOK opens question A
  beat 1 partly answers A, opens B ("it worked… for four days")
  beat 2 answers B, raises the stakes
  beat 3 the turn: what they assumed was wrong
PAYOFF lands A, hard, then stop
```

Re-hooks should come from the content, not from filler phrases. "And it worked. For four days." is a re-hook. "But here's where it gets crazy" is a filler phrase, and viewers can smell it.

## Structures that work (choose by content, not habit)

**Result → rewind → process → twist.** Show the outcome, jump back to the start, speed through the process (two or three big jumps, no filler), then add a detail that changes how the result reads. Good for tutorials, builds, AI tests and transformations. "Show the results first, then the process" lives here.

**Story ladder.** One-line situation → what someone wants → it goes wrong → it gets worse in a more specific way → turn → last line. Horror, life stories and client stories use this. Every rung should be more specific than the last. The final line reframes the first one, or is a callback to it.

**List with escalation.** Three items at most for under 30 seconds. Put the second-best first, the best last, and tease the best in the hook ("…the last one replaced three apps"). Number them on screen. Each item gets a quick proof, not a description.

**Test or challenge.** Rule → attempt → setback → second attempt → result. Keep the score or the stakes on screen. End on the result frame.

**Explainer as a mystery.** Open with the weird fact, the obvious explanation (wrong), the real one, and then the implication for *you*. Never "Today we'll learn about X."

**Take, or rant.** Claim → the proof that hurts → a concession that makes you credible ("yes, it's fine for…") → the claim restated harder, in new words. Built for comments.

**Skit or dialogue.** Situation in one line → escalation in three or four exchanges, each funnier or worse than the last → button (a callback or a reversal), then cut immediately. The joke is never explained.

**Loop.** Write the last line so it flows into the first frame. Replays count. Only use it when it's natural; a forced loop feels cheap.

## Pacing

- **First three seconds:** zero setup. No "so", no greeting, no context. Context goes in later, as half a sentence.
- **Speech rate:** about 2.5–3 words per second. Script length = target seconds × 2.7. 30 seconds is about 80 words; 45 seconds is about 120.
- **Length:** use the shortest that delivers the payoff. Rough bands: a joke or meme 7–20s; a tip or news item 20–40s; a story 35–75s. A longer video is fine if every beat earns its place.
- **Change something every 2–4 seconds:** a cut, zoom, angle, B-roll, on-screen text or graphic. Change on a meaning boundary (a new idea, a turn), not on a metronome.
- **Contrast creates impact.** Silence before the reveal. A held face after fast cuts. A whisper after loud.
- **On-screen captions:** burned-in subtitles, two to four words at a time, with key words highlighted. Most people watch muted at first.
- **The payoff gets room.** Give the reveal a beat to land (a held frame or a half-second pause), then end within a second or two.

## Endings

- **Best:** the payoff line itself, then cut. The video ends while the feeling is at its peak.
- **Button:** one short line after the payoff that tops it with a reversal or a callback ("Can I book my ticket on this?" "No, the trains are fake." "…Still faster."). If the last line *explains* anything, cut it.
- **Callback:** the last line echoes the first in a new light.
- **Loop:** the end runs into the start.
- **Comment trigger:** only a question with real disagreement or a fun personal answer: "Which one did you think was AI?", "What's your shower-argument person called?" Never "Thoughts?" or "Comment YES".
- **Follow ask:** when the next video is a clear promise: "Part two is who's actually responsible. Follow so you don't miss it." Series and part 2s are strong for building a following, as long as part 2 actually gets made.
- **Comment-keyword:** "Comment AGENT and I'll DM you the workflow." This is a good CTA for tutorials and builds when the thing being sent is real and useful. It grows comments, DMs and community, and surfaces leads.
- **Bad endings:** a summary, a moral, "and that's why…", "remember…", "follow for more", and anything after the payoff that isn't funnier than the payoff.

## Shares: why people send things

They send it because it says something about them or about the person they're sending it to: "this is so you", "we need to do this", "you were right", "look how accurate", "you'll find this useful". Write the video so that sentence is obvious, using a specific relatable detail, a usable tool or trick, a debate starter, or an emotional line worth screenshotting.
