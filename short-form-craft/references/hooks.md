# Hooks

A hook has to do two things in about a second and a half. It makes the viewer understand roughly what this is, and it makes them need the next sentence. Clarity without a question gets swiped as "got it". A question without clarity gets swiped as "huh?".

## Three layers, three jobs

| Layer | Job | Example (agency AI test) |
|---|---|---|
| **First frame** | Something worth looking at *before* any words: the result, a face mid-reaction, the weird object, motion | Two landing pages side by side |
| **Spoken line** | The tension: stakes, a contradiction, a confession | "One of these took our designer two days. The other took eleven minutes." |
| **Text on screen** | Context for people watching on mute, or a second hook | "client didn't know which was AI" |

If all three say the same thing, you've wasted two of them.

## Archetypes

Write hooks from at least five *different* archetypes, then pick. The examples set the bar for specificity; copy that level of detail, never the line itself.

**1. Result first.** Show the finished thing or end state, then rewind. This is the strongest default for tutorials, builds, transformations and tests.
- "I made this in eleven minutes with one prompt. Here's the prompt."
- [the finished hard edit's best frame] TEXT: "how this transition works"

**2. In the middle of it.** Open mid-scene or mid-sentence, as if they walked into the room late.
- "—and that's when the client asked for a refund. On a project we hadn't started."
- "There's a sign outside my Minecraft house that I didn't place."

**3. Specific contradiction.** Two true things that shouldn't both be true.
- "We cancelled ₹14,000 of software last month and got faster."
- "The most-watched scene in the show has no dialogue."

**4. Stakes or a bet.** Something can be won or lost, and the viewer wants to see which.
- "If she guesses which one's AI, I'm paying for dinner."
- "One heart. No armour. The village is on the other side of the ravine."

**5. Confession.** Admit something slightly embarrassing or honest.
- "I've won about four hundred arguments with my sister. In the shower."
- "We undercharged our first client so badly she tipped us."

**6. Precise recognition.** Name a micro-behaviour so exactly that the viewer feels caught. It has to be *this* specific. "If you overthink" is not a hook.
- "When something funny happens and your thumb opens their chat before your brain remembers."
- "If you've ever typed 'haha' with a completely straight face—"

**7. The claim.** A strong, defensible opinion delivered flatly, with no "I think" and no "unpopular opinion".
- "Stop using ChatGPT to write your emails. Use it to read them."
- "Walter White wasn't the villain of the first season. Skyler was the only one who saw it."

**8. Fresh news, with the "so what" attached.** It must be real and verified, and posted while it's still fresh.
- "Google made the thing you pay ₹1,600 a month for free yesterday. There's one catch."

**9. Curiosity object.** Point at a specific thing and ask about it.
- "Every Indian kitchen has this steel box and nobody knows it's supposed to be airtight."
- "This block has been in Minecraft since 2009 and has never done anything."

**10. Versus or comparison.** Two things side by side, with a judgement coming.
- "₹200 ChatGPT vs ₹0 ChatGPT. Same prompt. Watch the second one."

**11. Pure visual or sound.** No words: an impact, a cold dialogue line from a scene, silence before a drop. Mostly for edits.
- [black, one line of dialogue from the character] → beat drop on the first cut.

**12. Direct challenge to the viewer.** Get them to do something in their head right now.
- "Pause on the third frame. Tell me what's wrong with it."
- "You have five seconds to guess which one's the real Rolex."

## Hooks that are dead on arrival

These read as AI or as template creator-speak, and viewers have learned to swipe them:

"Have you ever wondered…", "Did you know…", "Nobody talks about this", "This will blow your mind", "Wait till the end", "Stop scrolling", "Let me tell you", "In today's video", "Here's why…" (on its own), "Unpopular opinion:", "POV:" followed by a generic situation, "X things you didn't know about Y", "This feels illegal to know", "Watch this before it gets deleted", "You're doing X wrong" (unless you then prove something genuinely surprising), "Imagine…", and opening with a greeting or your name.

A dead phrase can sometimes be resurrected if what follows it is outrageously specific. Default to avoiding them.

## Checks before choosing

- **Mute test:** with sound off, does the first frame plus the text still create a question?
- **Stranger test:** would someone who has never seen this account understand the first line?
- **Promise test:** does the video actually pay off this exact hook? A hook that wins the stop but betrays the promise gets swiped at second 4 and loses trust.
- **Read-aloud test:** does it sound like something a person would say to a friend? If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.
- **Speed:** the full premise should be clear within about 8–12 spoken words.
