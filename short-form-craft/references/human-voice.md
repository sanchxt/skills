# Writing like a person

Viewers clock AI-written scripts in seconds, even when they can't explain how. The cause usually isn't individual words. It's *shapes*: balanced sentences, tidy lists of three, abstract claims, and endings that sum things up. Swapping synonyms doesn't fix a shape. Rewrite the sentence around the concrete thing that happened.

## Human isn't quiet

Don't over-correct into soft, literary, wry minimalism. That reads as "writing", and it's boring on a feed. Real creators who blow up are *energetic, direct, and specific*. They talk like they just found something out and can't wait to tell you. Aim for confident and punchy. Save understatement for single lines where it lands a joke or a gut-punch.

## How real people talk on camera

- **Concrete nouns:** the steel tiffin, the 3:12 AM message, the ₹499 plan, the grey hoodie.
- **Uneven rhythm.** One long sentence that runs on a bit because they're excited. Then a short one. Fragment.
- **Reactions and asides:** "which, fine", "I know", "anyway", "no, seriously".
- **Self-correction:** "it took ten minutes, eleven, whatever".
- **Pointing at the screen:** "this. this bit right here."
- **Jokes that undercut:** "we're professionals" while something is visibly on fire.
- **Two-person dialogue:** people interrupt, react without finishing, and say "wait, what?". They don't take turns delivering balanced paragraphs.
- **Behaviour instead of feelings:** "I typed it, deleted it, typed it again" says more than "I felt anxious".
- **Trusting the viewer.** Don't explain the joke or the feeling. Stop one line earlier than feels safe.

## AI tells: find these and kill them

**Structural tells (the worst ones):**
- "It's not X, it's Y." / "Not because X, but because Y." / "Not X. Not Y. Just Z."
- Lists of three everywhere, especially of adjectives or abstract nouns.
- A rhetorical question answered immediately: "The result? Chaos." "The catch? There isn't one."
- Colon or em-dash reveals in every other line.
- Every sentence the same length. Every paragraph the same shape.
- An ending that summarises, moralises or "reminds" ("Remember: your peace matters.").
- Restating the hook in different words before getting going.
- Generic "we all" / "we've all been there" universalising.
- Profundity that means nothing: "Sometimes the hardest goodbyes are the ones we never say."

**Word-level tells:** journey, navigate, landscape, game-changer, unlock, elevate, level up, delve, realm, testament, crucial, seamless, effortless, harness, empower, transform, "the power of", "in today's world", "here's the thing", "let that sink in", "and honestly?", "let's be real", "the best part?", "truly", "incredibly", "absolutely".

Also: "healing isn't linear", "protect your peace", "your feelings are valid", "you are enough" (unless genuinely used ironically), "main character energy", "hits different".

## Before and after

**Feelings / relatable**
> ✗ "Sometimes the hardest goodbyes are the ones we never get to say. Healing isn't linear, it's a journey of learning to let go."
>
> ✓ "I still have his number saved as 'do not answer'. I've never had to not answer. He hasn't called in eight months."

**Tech**
> ✗ "AI is revolutionising the way we work. Here are three game-changing tools that will transform your productivity!"

> ✓ "he was 19 here"

## The read-aloud pass

Read every line aloud, or imagine saying it to a friend across a table. If you wouldn't say it that way, rewrite it. Watch for tongue-twisters, three sentences in a row starting with the same word, accidental rhymes, and pronouns where it isn't clear who "he" is.
