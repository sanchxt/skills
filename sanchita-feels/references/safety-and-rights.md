# Safety, accuracy, and rights

Read this for any mental-health claim, sensitive topic, meditation/wellness instruction, user-submitted story, or third-party media.

## Role boundary

SanchitaFeels may offer relatable reflection and general education. It must not diagnose, treat, or imply clinical authority the creator has not stated. Never invent credentials or use titles such as psychologist, therapist, counselor, or doctor without verification.

- Use "may," "can," "often," or "some people" when contexts vary.
- Distinguish a common reaction from a disorder.
- Never infer that a partner is a narcissist, avoidant, abusive, traumatized, or mentally ill from one behavior.
- Do not tell viewers to stop medication, therapy, or professional care.
- Do not offer personalized diagnosis or therapy in comments or DMs.
- General disclaimers do not rescue inaccurate or unsafe content; fix the claim itself.

APA guidance says general information should be distinguished from individual treatment and that diagnosis or therapy should not be provided through social media: <https://www.apa.org/research-practice/working-with-media/digital-social-media>. APA also warns that inaccurate mental-health content and expansive definitions can contribute to self-diagnosis and harm: <https://www.apa.org/topics/journalism-facts/misinformation-mental-health>.

## Evidence and metaphor

Label content clearly:

- "Evidence-informed explanation" only when the specific claim is supported.
- "One possible lens" for plausible but non-exclusive interpretations.
- "Internet metaphor" for burnt-toast-style meaning-making.
- "Cultural/spiritual belief" for red-string or invisible-string traditions where applicable.
- "Personal reflection" when sharing a supplied lived experience.

Do not use neuroscience decoration—dopamine, nervous system, trauma response, cortisol, attachment—to make a generic relationship opinion sound scientific.

## Sensitive and crisis content

For suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, abuse, or acute crisis:

- do not use sensational hooks, romantic imagery, explicit methods, locations, notes, or graphic visuals;
- do not reduce suicide or self-harm to a breakup or single cause;
- center hope, coping, help-seeking, and recovery;
- provide current, location-appropriate crisis resources after verifying them live; if location is unknown, recommend local emergency services and a reputable global directory rather than guessing a number;
- if a user asks for content that could endanger viewers, explain the issue and offer a safer angle.

WHO's 2023 media guidance recommends accurate help information, stories of coping and recovery, and avoiding method details, sensationalism, glamorization, and oversimplification: <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076846>.

Meditation and grounding can be offered as optional wellbeing practices, not cures. Use invitational wording; acknowledge that a practice may not suit everyone.

## Privacy and community

- Remove names, handles, photos, timestamps, locations, and distinctive details from follower submissions unless explicit permission covers publication.
- Do not present a real person's DM as a case study.
- Never crowdsource a diagnosis.
- Moderate comments that encourage self-harm, harassment, or medical misinformation; give crisis responses priority over engagement goals.
- Avoid prompts that pressure viewers to disclose trauma publicly.

## Visual rights

Pinterest is a discovery/save platform, not a blanket stock license. Pinterest says permission may need to be obtained from the copyright holder: <https://help.pinterest.com/en/article/copyright>.

Use only:

- creator-shot footage;
- properly licensed stock under terms that cover social/commercial use as needed;
- public-domain or suitable Creative Commons material with required attribution;
- direct permission documented by the creator;
- built-in remix/stitch assets used within platform terms.

Do not download and repost random clips, crop watermarks, or assume credit equals permission. Keep a simple rights log with asset URL, creator, license, download date, required attribution, and proof/screenshot.

## Audio rights and cross-posting

Licenses differ between Instagram and YouTube. Keep a clean export with voice/dialogue and no platform-licensed track, then add each platform's cleared audio natively. For sponsored or commercial content, re-check whether the platform library license covers the use. If unclear, use original or separately licensed audio.

YouTube states that reused content without significant original commentary or modification can affect monetization and that copyright still applies even when a transformed work meets reused-content rules: <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392>.

## Correction protocol

If a published claim is materially wrong:

1. Stop recommending or reposting it.
2. Verify the correction with strong sources.
3. Correct transparently in the same place when possible; do not quietly preserve misleading reach.
4. Decide whether to edit, annotate, archive, or remove based on harm and platform capability.
5. Record the failure pattern so future scripts avoid it.
