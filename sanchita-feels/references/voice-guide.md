# Voice and writing guide

The voice is emotionally intelligent, grounded, observant, feminine without stereotyping, and quietly confident. It should sound like a real person thinking clearly beside the viewer—not a therapist performing authority, a motivational poster, or a synthetic "healing journey" narrator.

## Signature qualities

- Name micro-behaviors: deleting a draft, checking a story view, rehearsing a reply, taking the long route home, keeping the last photo.
- Let one unexpected but defensible sentence carry the video.
- Use warmth without babying the audience.
- Make room for uncertainty: "can," "may," "often," and "for some people" are useful when the evidence or context is not universal.
- Offer agency without blaming the viewer.
- Keep the language spoken, not essay-like.
- Allow gentle poetry, but anchor it in an object, action, or body sensation.

## Anti-slop rules

Avoid:

- generic openings: "Have you ever wondered..." or "We have all been there";
- stacked abstractions: "embrace your authentic healing journey";
- fake profundity: a vague quote over unrelated rain footage;
- rhythmically identical three-item lists in every script;
- repeating the hook in new words before adding value;
- overusing "not because X, but because Y";
- declaring hidden motives as fact;
- absolute advice such as "If they wanted to, they would" without context;
- moralizing attachment styles or turning a label into a personality verdict.

## Useful tonal modes

### Close-friend observation

"The hardest part of being ghosted is not always losing them. Sometimes it is having nowhere to put the version of the story you were still building."

### Clean explanation

"Your brain dislikes unfinished patterns. That can help explain why ambiguity keeps pulling your attention back—but it does not mean one psychological effect explains every breakup."

### Gentle boundary

"You can ask once for clarity. You do not have to audition for an answer after that."

### Theory honesty

"The burnt toast theory is a comforting internet metaphor: a small delay might redirect your day. Keep the comfort. Just do not mistake it for evidence that every inconvenience was planned for you."

### Calming reset

"Before you send the message, put both feet on the floor. Name three things you can see. Then decide from the calmer version of the moment."

These are calibration examples, not reusable templates. Do not copy them by default.

## Perspective and disclosure

Never fabricate the creator's lived experience. Use "I" only for facts the user supplies or for clearly framed creative lines the user approves. Otherwise use observational language. Do not assume all women date men, want relationships, experience emotions in the same way, or share one cultural context.

## Calls to action

One CTA is usually enough. Match it to the value:

- share: "Send this to the friend who keeps blaming herself for the silence."
- save: "Keep this sentence for the moment you want to ask for clarity."
- comment: "Which part is harder for you: the silence or the unanswered why?"
- follow: "I make short videos for feelings that are hard to explain."

Avoid engagement bait, guilt, false scarcity, or "comment YES if you agree."

## Language adaptation

If the user wants Hindi, Hinglish, or another language, preserve natural spoken rhythm and cultural nuance rather than translating line by line. Ask for the desired register only when it materially changes the script (e.g. urban Hinglish, Hindi-first, or international English). Provide pronunciation or subtitle variants when useful.
