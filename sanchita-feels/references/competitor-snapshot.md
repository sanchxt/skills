# Competitor and adjacent-creator snapshot

Research date: 2026-09-06. This is a benchmark snapshot, not a permanent leaderboard. Re-check live activity and recent posts before making current claims.

## What the benchmark set shows

The recurring growth pattern is not "talk about mental health." It is a recognizable content unit: one audience problem, a repeatable voice/format, and an immediate payoff. Large accounts also demonstrate what SanchitaFeels should avoid: generic therapy-speak, false certainty, diagnosis bait, and advice detached from evidence or context.

### Dr Julie Smith — accessible psychology through visual demonstration

- Channels: <https://www.instagram.com/drjulie/> and <https://www.youtube.com/@DrJulie>
- Positioning: clinical psychologist translating adult mental health and human psychology into short, accessible lessons. Her site says she began short videos in 2019 after nearly a decade in practice: <https://drjulie.co.uk/about>.
- Growth evidence: in an interview, she described early YouTube attempts as poor, then said very short TikTok videos began growing within weeks; the audience response emphasized usefulness and encouragement to seek therapy: <https://www.psychologies.co.uk/dr-julie-smith-interview/>.
- Current-activity signal at research time: CreatorDB reported recent YouTube and Instagram uploads and materially above-category engagement, but third-party stats remain directional: <https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/drjulie/>.
- What appears to work: concrete props/visual metaphors, direct problem-first hooks, confident but accessible explanation, and a credential-backed trust signal.
- Do not copy: lab-coat authority, set pieces, or exact demonstrations. SanchitaFeels can borrow the principle "make the invisible visible" using everyday objects and clearly sourced claims.
- What did not work / limit: the creator's account of early long-form experiments suggests format-platform mismatch. Shortness alone was not the lesson; compressed useful education suited discovery. This is a creator report, not a controlled experiment.

### Nedra Glover Tawwab — signature, saveable boundary language

- Channel: <https://www.instagram.com/nedratawwab/>
- Positioning: therapist and author focused on boundaries and relationships; "#NedraNuggets" creates a searchable signature format.
- Growth evidence: TIME reported that she joined Instagram in 2017, built a large audience by normalizing difficult feelings and speaking in culturally familiar language, and deliberately keeps content informational rather than diagnostic: <https://time.com/6968404/nedra-glover-tawwab/>.
- Current-activity signal at research time: CreatorDB's July 2026 snapshot reported roughly 6.5 Instagram posts per week, a recent upload, and strong engagement relative to its stated category median: <https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/nedra-glover-tawwab-therapist/>. Use the provider's methodology and treat figures as time-bound: <https://creatordb.app/methodology/>.
- What appears to work: concise standalone lines, one owned topic, consistent series naming, language viewers can save or send, cultural specificity, and a newsletter path for deeper material.
- What may not transfer: static concise insights from a known expert can work differently for a zero-follower personality account. SanchitaFeels should add face, voice, scene, or story rather than becoming a quotation tile feed.
- Safety lesson: avoid personal feedback and diagnoses; point individualized needs toward local professionals. This trust boundary is part of the brand, not legal fine print.

### Tam Kaur — aspirational big-sister identity and creator-led transformation

- Channels: <https://www.instagram.com/tamkaur/> and <https://www.youtube.com/@tamkaur>
- Positioning: female self-love, confidence, growth, breakup recovery, and aspirational identity.
- Growth evidence: a 2023 interview title documents growth from roughly 2,000 to over 700,000 YouTube subscribers in ten months: <https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/121-tam-kaur-i-grew-from-2000-to-700-000-subscribers/id1517301518?i=1000635514336>. A 2024 profile described growth past one million and highlights her creator presence and self-confidence story: <https://www.creatorhandbook.net/tam-kaur-wants-you-to-overcome-your-biggest-obstacle-yourself/>.
- What appears to work: a strong "older sister" relationship, visible creator identity, transformation framing, direct titles, and continuity across video, podcast, and book/community offers.
- What did not work / limit: the profile reports that rapid growth multiplied roles and made it harder to preserve strategic focus and content quality. For SanchitaFeels, four daily posts need production tiers and a reusable footage/research system.
- Do not copy: luxury-coded aspiration or absolutes if they do not match the creator's real life. The transferable asset is a coherent personal voice.

### Psychology with Dr. Ana — evidence, nuance, and internet-discourse response

- Channels/site: <https://www.youtube.com/@AnaPsychology> and <https://www.dranayudin.com/my-videos>
- Positioning: evidence-based public psychology education about relationships, personality, pop psychology, and current internet claims.
- Activity signal at research time: her site says she has made weekly content since 2020, and a current YouTube mirror showed multiple uploads within days/weeks in 2026: <https://www.dranayudin.com/my-videos> and <https://invidious.logicism.tv/channel/UCOxhlNofmYH-MRC36TURUGw>.
- What appears to work: reacting to current discourse, challenging simplistic empowerment or attachment claims, titles built around a real disagreement/question, and explicit educational-not-therapy boundaries.
- What may not transfer: long-form nuance and critique cannot always compress safely into 20 seconds. SanchitaFeels can use a short hook and make one bounded point, then link a source or follow-up instead of pretending to settle a complex debate.

## Cross-competitor deductions

These are reasoned inferences from the public evidence, not proven causal claims:

- Own a repeatable phrase or series, but let the viewer problem lead.
- Face and voice deepen parasocial trust; saveable text and tools increase distribution opportunities.
- Specificity beats generic positivity. "The text you can send after two days of silence" has more utility than "choose yourself."
- Cultural and personal familiarity can create connection, but must be genuine rather than manufactured.
- Fast growth adds operational pressure; a high-volume account needs a quality floor, research queue, and batching.
- Credibility is a growth asset. Clearly separating education, opinion, and metaphor differentiates the account in a niche crowded with overclaiming.

## SanchitaFeels whitespace

Combine the emotional specificity and aesthetic intimacy of a relatable creator with the labeling discipline of a careful educator. A promising signature is:

> "what this can feel like / what may be happening / one sentence or step you can use"

Make the creator's calm face, voice, original outdoor footage, and honest uncertainty recognizable. The account should not impersonate a therapist; it should become trusted for explaining what is known, what is only a lens, and what the viewer can safely try next.
