# Production-ready Reel and Short package

Use this structure when delivering concrete video concepts. Scale detail to the request, but do not omit items that the user needs to film or upload.

## Per-concept package

### 1. Concept card

- Working concept and series/pillar
- Exact viewer moment and emotional promise
- Primary goal: completion, replay, share, save, comment, profile visit, or follow
- Recommended length and effort: low, medium, or anchor
- Content label when relevant: personal reflection, licensed professional insight, evidence-informed education, practical communication, meditation, folklore/spiritual belief, metaphor, or unverified social trend

### 2. Packaging

- Cover text: 3–7 readable words
- Spoken/text hook: one sentence that begins immediately
- Visual first frame: visible action, expression, prop, motion, or large text; never "Hi guys, today..."
- Open loop: what the viewer expects to learn or feel by the end

Useful hook families:

- **Specific behavior:** "If you keep reopening their chat even though you know they will not reply..."
- **Contradiction:** "Sometimes closure is not one final conversation. It is noticing the conversation has already ended."
- **Uncomfortable recognition:** "You may not miss them. You may miss the version of you that still had hope."
- **Two-sided tension:** "Ghosting hurts—but sometimes the person disappearing is avoiding their own shame, not proving your worth."
- **Search-intent question:** "Why does being ghosted feel physically painful?"
- **Theory audit:** "The burnt toast theory is comforting—but it is not a psychological theory."
- **Borrowable line:** "If you want clarity without chasing, send this once."

Do not use empty alarmism ("Nobody tells you this"), fake universality ("This is exactly why"), or diagnostic bait ("Five signs your ex is a narcissist").

### 3. Script variations

Provide three variations for one to three concepts, and at least two for larger batches unless the user asks for concise brainstorming.

Make the variations structurally different:

- **A — intimate:** first-person or close-friend voice, emotionally warm.
- **B — crisp:** faster, direct, high-retention explanation.
- **C — poetic/visual or dialogue:** image-rich, cinematic, or a two-voice exchange.

For each script:

- write exact speakable words;
- use contractions and varied sentence lengths;
- put the most concrete line first;
- add beat or pause markers only where performance benefits;
- keep one core point; remove throat-clearing and summaries;
- finish with an earned final line, then a natural CTA if needed.

Read the script aloud mentally. Remove phrases such as "in today's fast-paced world," "it's important to remember," "let's dive in," "here's the thing," "navigate," "journey," and "you are not alone" unless the specific line genuinely sounds like this creator.

### 4. Beat sheet and visuals

Give a timestamped plan. A common 25–35 second shape is:

- 0:00–0:02: visual and verbal hook
- 0:02–0:08: recognizable moment
- 0:08–0:20: explanation or turn
- 0:20–0:28: action/reframe
- 0:28–end: resonant close or one CTA

For every beat specify:

- camera/B-roll (face, hands, walking, window light, journaling, phone action, nature, prop);
- on-screen words, kept shorter than narration;
- cut, zoom, pattern interrupt, or silence;
- subtitle emphasis words;
- audio behavior: original voice, quiet bed, beat drop, or intentional silence.

If using aesthetic B-roll, say whether it should be creator-shot, licensed stock, public domain, or platform-remixable. Never write "grab a Pinterest clip."

### 5. Caption and engagement

Captions should add a layer instead of transcribing the voiceover. Use:

- one-line continuation or counterpoint;
- 2–5 short lines of useful nuance;
- one honest prompt with a low-friction response.

Good prompts invite lived experience without demanding disclosure: "What helped you stop checking the chat?" "Would this sentence feel kind or too final?" "Save this for the day you want to text them again." Avoid "Thoughts?" and avoid asking vulnerable viewers to reveal diagnoses or trauma publicly.

### 6. Instagram package

- Searchable cover line
- Caption
- 5–10 natural-language keywords to use across speech, on-screen text, caption, and alt text; do not keyword-stuff
- Zero to five hashtags, chosen for topical relevance and series discovery rather than reach folklore
- Story follow-up or pinned-comment idea when useful

### 7. YouTube Shorts package

- Title: clear promise with curiosity; usually concise and human
- One or two description lines with natural keywords
- Zero to three highly relevant hashtags if useful; stay within the account-wide maximum of five
- Pinned comment or related-video bridge when available

YouTube says performance can be understood through appeal, engagement, and satisfaction, that the opening should immediately deliver the packaging promise, and that tags are not essential for discovery. Build for those outcomes rather than stuffing metadata: <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650>

### 8. Why it can work and what to test

State the mechanism as a hypothesis, not a promise:

- why the first frame may stop the scroll;
- where recognition, tension, value, and payoff occur;
- why someone would share, save, or comment;
- one A/B variable: hook wording, face vs. B-roll, length, final line, title, or CTA. Change one major variable at a time.

### 9. Sources and confidence

For factual psychology, list the claim, source link, access date, and confidence/limitations. Sources do not need to clutter a poetic metaphor post, but the post must be labeled accurately. Never attach a study citation to a claim that the study did not test.

## Music suggestion format

Music status changes quickly. Search the live platform or current reputable sources before saying a sound is trending. For each suggestion give:

- track/audio name and artist/uploader;
- platform and direct link if accessible;
- why its mood and edit points fit;
- whether speech remains intelligible;
- rights route: Instagram licensed library, YouTube Shorts library, user-owned/original, or separately licensed;
- a no-trend fallback described by mood, BPM range, and instrumentation.

Do not quote lyrics beyond a very short fragment. Do not promise that a song licensed on one platform is cleared on the other. Export a clean master without copyrighted music, then add platform-cleared audio natively when appropriate.
