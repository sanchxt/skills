# Attention: hooks, structure and pacing for voice-led motion graphics

How to make a stranger stop, stay to the end, and send the video on. This file is the motion-graphics layer on top of `short-form-craft` (read its hooks.md, retention.md, human-voice.md and critic.md as well). Use it when choosing an idea, writing the script and storyboarding. **Every script passes the checklist at the bottom before it goes to the storyboard.**

Researched 2026-11-18. Platforms publish signals, not weights, so the percentage benchmarks below come from vendor analyses and are **directional only**. Check them against our own numbers in `log.md` once we have some. Re-check this file every few months.

## What the platforms reward

| Platform | Confirmed signals | Rough benchmarks (unofficial) |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | Mosseri has publicly named **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**, with sends (DM shares) mattering more for reaching non-followers. Original content is favoured: accounts that mostly repost others' content can stop being recommended. AI-made content isn't down-ranked for being AI, but it should be labelled. | About half of viewers can be gone by second 3 |
| YouTube Shorts | Each Short goes to a small test audience first, and **viewed vs swiped away** plus how much of it people watch decide whether it spreads. "Engaged views" is the stricter view count in Analytics. | Swipe-away under about 25–35% is healthy. Retention above about 65% (under 30 s) or 50% (30–60 s) seems to get pushed wider |
| Meta ads | **Hook rate** = 3-second views ÷ impressions. Captions and mute-first design are standard. | Hook rate above 25–35% is good, and under 15% means the opening is broken. Hooks wear out in about 2–4 weeks at scale, so keep fresh variants ready. Prospecting cuts 6–15 s, demos 15–30 s |

What this means for us: **the first 1.5 seconds decide whether the video gets seen at all, completion decides whether it spreads, and sends decide whether non-followers ever see it.** Every choice below serves one of those three.

## The idea comes first

A polished video of a small idea still gets swiped. Before scripting, the idea has to be **big for a business owner who has never heard of us**: it touches their money, customers, reputation or time, or it looks impossible, or they can try it on themselves right now. A feature demo ("our workflow does X") is a small idea. "Your missed calls cost you this much — work it out with me" is a big one built around the same feature.

**Vary the format across a batch.** No two videos in a batch use the same container, and never post the same container back to back unless it's a proven series. Formats that suit voice-led motion graphics:

| Format | Why it holds attention | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Viewer challenge / try it now** | The viewer does something in their head or on their phone, so they're involved from second 1 | "Open ChatGPT and ask for the best dentist in your city. Are you in it?" |
| **Do the maths with me** | A number builds on screen, and the total is the payoff | Missed calls × customer value × 52 |
| **Quiz / spot it** | A countdown plus a reveal makes people stay and comment their answer | "One reply is a person, one is AI. Five seconds." |
| **Tier list / ranking** | Every placement is a mini-payoff, and people argue in the comments | What's worth automating, S → D |
| **Real experiment with a scoreboard** | Stakes plus a running score; the result is unknown until the end | "We messaged 10 businesses at 11 PM" |
| **Story ladder with a rewind** | A timeline that gets worse, then rewinds and replays fixed | One enquiry lost, then the same one saved |
| **Five-second test / before-after** | The viewer fails the test, then sees the fix | A homepage nobody can read in 5 s |
| **Result → how it's built** | An impossible-looking result first, then 3–5 fast steps | Voice note → PDF quote in 30 s |
| **Take** | A flat opinion, proof, a concession, then the claim restated harder | "Switch your chatbot off tonight" |
| **Offer ad** | Pain → offer → proof → CTA, with 3 hook variants | Lead Responder |

## The first 1.5 seconds (the stop)

- **Frame 0 is a finished, moving composition.** No fade-in, no logo, no title card, no build-up. Something is already happening: a notification landing, a number counting, a chat typing, a countdown running. That frame is also the cover.
- **The voice starts in the first 0.1–0.2 s.** Trim every bit of leading silence and breath, and start the VO on frame 0–5.
- **A sound hit in the first 0.3 s** (pop, tick, whoosh) under the first visual change. Keep it quiet and short.
- **Three layers, three jobs** (from short-form-craft): the visual makes you look, the spoken line opens the question, and the on-screen text (≤ 7 words, centre safe zone, big) gives muted viewers the hook or a second hook. If all three say the same thing, two of them are wasted.
- **The mute test is mandatory.** Most viewers start muted, and Feed and ads are mostly silent. With sound off, frame 0 plus the hook text must still create a question.
- **Clarity plus a question.** Within about 8–12 spoken words the viewer knows roughly what this is *and* needs the next sentence. Being clear without a question gets "got it" and a swipe; a question without clarity gets "huh?" and a swipe.

**Hook shapes that work in motion graphics** (pick from at least 3 different ones, and write 3–5 options per video):
- **Viewer challenge:** "Open your calculator." / "You've got five seconds."
- **Timestamp, in the middle of it:** "7:14 PM. Someone comments 'price?' on your reel."
- **Number slam:** a big number on frame 0, then what it means.
- **Precise recognition:** quote the exact thing they've seen ("Sorry, I didn't understand that. Please choose from the options below.")
- **Result first:** the impossible-looking end state, then "here's what's behind it".
- **Stakes or experiment:** "We messaged ten businesses at 11 PM."
- **Flat claim:** "Most businesses automate the wrong thing first."

Dead hooks: anything starting with a logo, a greeting, "Did you know", "Have you ever", "Stop scrolling", "In today's video", "Imagine", "Here's why…" on its own, or "X tips for Y".

## Seconds 2–6 (the hold)

The hook is a promise. By about second 6 the premise is fully clear and the first piece of the answer has landed, **while a smaller question is open**. The most common motion-graphics mistake here is a slow "explain the context" beat. Slip context in as half a sentence later.

## The middle (the stay)

- **Answer one question, open a smaller one.** Each beat pays something off and leaves something open. If the viewer can predict the ending, they leave.
- **Re-hook from the content about every 8–10 s:** a number landing, a turn ("Nobody here was lazy"), a rewind, a reveal, a countdown. Never a filler phrase like "but here's where it gets crazy".
- **Put the strongest visual moment around the 35–50% mark**, where retention usually sags. That's where the music drop, the big reveal or the rewind goes. Don't spend it all in the first five seconds.
- **Show progress** on lists, steps and tests: "2/4", a tier row filling up, a timer, a scoreboard. Visible progress keeps people watching to see it finish.
- **Something changes on screen every 2–4 s, on a meaning boundary** (a new idea, a number, a turn), not on a timer. One hero motion per beat (motion.md).
- **Contrast does the work:** a held frame after fast cuts, silence before the reveal, one word on screen after a busy layout.
- **Let the viewer do something:** a pause to guess, a countdown, a calculation, "be honest". Participation holds attention better than information does.

## The ending (completion, comments, sends)

- **Land the payoff, give it about half a second to land, and end within 1–2 s.** No recap, no moral, no "so that's why".
- **The CTA is one line and ≤ 3 s,** on the end card. Pick one job: a comment keyword for something real we'll send, a comment question with real disagreement (A or B, "what's in your S tier"), or a DM keyword for an offer. Never two CTAs.
- **Loop when it's natural:** the last frame flows into the first (the rewind format, the calculator resetting), so replays count.
- **Write the send line before recording:** who sends this to whom, and what do they type? ("are we in this?", "this is literally our chatbot", "do the maths for the shop"). If there's no honest answer, sharpen the idea.

## Length and words

- Use the shortest length that delivers the payoff. Organic reels **20–40 s** (under 30 s whenever it works), ads 15–30 s with a 6–15 s cut, and walkthroughs longer only when every beat earns it.
- Speech runs at about **2.7 words per second**: 30 s ≈ 80 words and 40 s ≈ 110. Cut 15–20% after the first draft.
- A pause that's part of the format (a quiz countdown, "Go." then 3 s) counts toward the length, and earns it.

## Sound

- Design mute-first, and reward sound-on. Captions are always on (captions.md); key numbers and words go in the accent colour.
- The music drop lands on the reveal at mid-video, the outro sits under the CTA, and there's a short silence or a filter-down right before the payoff line.
- One soft SFX per visual change (pop, tick, whoosh, ding), well under the voice. No constant whoosh bed.

## Testing hooks

- Record **3 hooks per video** (different shapes, same body). For ads, make each one a separate composition. For organic, post the strongest, and test the others as Instagram **Trial Reels** (shown to non-followers first) or as reposted variants later.
- Read the numbers: 3-second hold or hook rate (is the stop working?), average % watched and the retention curve (where do people leave?), sends and saves per reach (is it worth sharing?). Log them in `log.md` with the hook used, and write the lesson there.

## Checklist before storyboarding (every script)

| Check | Pass when |
|---|---|
| Idea size | A business owner who's never heard of us cares about the outcome in second 1 |
| Format | Different from the rest of the batch and from the last post |
| Frame 0 | Already moving, says something, works as the cover |
| Mute test | Frame 0 + hook text creates a question with no sound |
| Hook | Premise clear in ≤ 12 spoken words; 3–5 options written, 3 recorded |
| Hold | No context-only beat before second 6 |
| Re-hooks | A new number, turn, reveal or countdown about every 8–10 s |
| Midpoint | The biggest visual moment sits around 35–50% |
| Ending | Ends within 1–2 s of the payoff; one CTA ≤ 3 s |
| Send line | Written down: who sends it to whom, and what they type |
| Length | ≤ 40 s organic / ≤ 30 s ad, unless every beat earns more |
| Truth | Every number, result and test is real or labelled as an example/demo; CTA promises are things we'll actually do |
