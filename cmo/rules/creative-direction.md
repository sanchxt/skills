# Creative Direction

## Purpose

Provide detailed, distinctive visual direction for every piece of content. The visual is the first thing someone sees — before the hook, before the caption. A distinctive visual stops the scroll. A generic one gets ignored.

Every post should have a clear creative vision. No templates. No "clean and professional." No generic stock-photo energy.

## Before Starting

1. Read `.growth/brand-profile.md` for brand colors, typography, and visual preferences
2. Read `.growth/voice-library.md` for established visual patterns that work (if exists)
3. Consider the specific platform's visual requirements and culture
4. Consider the content type (carousel needs different direction than a reel or static post)

## Creative Brief Structure

Every content piece needs a creative brief with these elements:

### 1. Visual Concept

A one-line creative direction that captures the essence. Be bold and specific.

**Strong concepts:**
- "Split-screen before/after with halftone grain texture, warm amber tones, oversized serif numbers"
- "Neo-brutalist carousel: hot pink and electric lime on matte black, thick borders, Impact font, deliberately 'ugly' in a beautiful way"
- "Minimalist flat lay with dramatic hard shadows, desaturated earth palette with single pop of brand orange"
- "Collage-style mixed media: cut-out photography layered with hand-drawn arrows and marker annotations, kraft paper background"
- "Cinematic portrait: shallow depth of field, golden hour side-lighting, film grain, no text overlay — let the image breathe"
- "Data visualization as art: infographic with risograph-style limited color printing, slight misregistration, textured paper"
- "Retro magazine editorial: think 70s Vogue — serif headlines, warm film tones, editorial layout with generous margins"

**Weak concepts (NEVER do this):**
- "Professional looking image" (meaningless)
- "Nice design with brand colors" (not a direction)
- "Clean and modern" (every generic design claims this)
- "Eye-catching graphic" (says nothing)

### 2. Visual Style Vocabulary

Use these as a palette to mix and match. Combine styles for unique results. Never default to the same style twice in a row.

#### Photographic Styles
- **Cinematic**: Dramatic lighting, shallow depth of field, film grain, widescreen framing, color-graded like a film scene
- **Documentary/Raw**: Unpolished, authentic moments, natural light, slight imperfection that adds character
- **Editorial**: Magazine-quality, styled and deliberate, every element placed with intention
- **Lifestyle**: Aspirational, natural context, product-in-use, warm and inviting
- **Flat lay**: Overhead, organized chaos or strict symmetry, curated objects telling a story
- **Portrait-forward**: Human faces, real emotion, eye contact, connection through the screen
- **Street photography**: Candid, urban, energetic, caught-in-the-moment feel
- **Product/Still life**: Dramatic lighting on product, studio quality, textures amplified

#### Graphic Design Styles
- **Neo-brutalism**: Bold colors, thick black borders, raw typography, intentionally rough, 3D-ish shadow effects, looks like a Figma prototype with personality
- **Minimalism**: Extreme whitespace, one focal point, restrained palette, every pixel intentional — less is more but what remains is perfect
- **Maximalism**: Layered, textured, colorful, pattern-on-pattern, controlled chaos, overwhelming in a deliberate way
- **Swiss/International**: Grid-based, clean helvetica-style typography, geometric composition, information hierarchy
- **Retro/Vintage**: Era-specific color grading and typography (specify which era: 60s psychedelic, 70s earth tones, 80s neon, 90s grunge, Y2K chrome)
- **Y2K/Futuristic retro**: Chrome effects, metallic gradients, bubble fonts, translucent elements, iridescent surfaces
- **Art Deco**: Geometric patterns, gold accents, luxury feel, symmetry, ornamental borders
- **Vaporwave**: Pastel pink/purple/blue gradients, Greek statues, retro tech imagery, dreamy digital nostalgia
- **Organic/Natural**: Flowing shapes, earth tones, botanical illustrations, watercolor washes, hand-crafted feel
- **Memphis**: Bold geometric shapes, primary colors, squiggles, playful patterns, 80s Italian design
- **Bauhaus**: Primary colors, geometric shapes, functional beauty, less decoration more structure
- **Japanese minimal**: Wabi-sabi imperfection, natural materials, asymmetric balance, muted tones, zen simplicity
- **Punk/Zine**: Cut-and-paste collage, ransom-note typography, photocopied textures, DIY energy, anarchic layouts
- **Corporate Memphis subversion**: Take the generic flat illustration style and make it weird — surreal proportions, unexpected colors, absurdist scenarios

#### Texture & Effects
- **Halftone**: Newspaper dot pattern, retro print feel, great for adding tactile quality to flat designs
- **Dither**: Pixel-level gradient patterns, digital nostalgia, limited color palette constraint
- **Grain/Noise**: Film grain overlay, analog warmth, adds depth and authenticity to digital images
- **Risograph**: Layered limited-color prints, slight misregistration between layers, indie press aesthetic
- **Duotone**: Two-color treatment, dramatic and bold, great for portraits and product shots
- **Gradient mesh**: Complex multi-color gradients, depth and movement, liquid feel
- **Glitch**: Digital distortion, VHS tracking lines, pixel sorting, corrupted data aesthetic
- **Watercolor bleed**: Soft organic edges, paint bleeding into white space, handmade feel
- **Cut-out/Collage**: Mixed media layering, magazine clippings, different textures and scales juxtaposed
- **Paper texture**: Kraft paper, cardboard, torn edges, stamps, handmade artisanal feel
- **Glass morphism**: Frosted glass panels, transparency and blur, floating layered cards
- **Claymorphism**: Soft 3D surfaces, rounded corners, clay-like inner shadows, playful depth
- **Chromatic aberration**: RGB color channel splitting, prismatic edge effects, optical distortion
- **Emboss/Deboss**: Pressed-into-surface effect, tactile luxury feel, subtle dimensionality
- **Noise gradient**: Grainy gradients that feel organic, trending aesthetic for modern brands
- **Solarization**: Inverted tones in highlights/shadows, psychedelic darkroom effect

#### Typography Approaches
- **Oversized display**: One word fills the frame, massive visual impact, typography AS the visual
- **Handwritten/Script**: Personal, authentic warmth, like a note from a friend
- **Monospace/Technical**: Data-driven credibility, coder aesthetic, modern and precise
- **Mixed weights**: Dramatic contrast between thin and ultra-bold in the same composition
- **Kinetic text**: Text as the primary visual element, animated for reels (scale, reveal, bounce, typewriter)
- **Stacked/Compressed**: Tight leading, text-as-texture, powerful wall of words
- **Type-on-image**: Text integrated with and responding to the photography beneath it
- **Redacted/Highlight**: Key words highlighted, crossed out, or circled as if edited by hand
- **3D/Extruded**: Letters with depth, shadow, dimension — physical presence on screen
- **Variable font animation**: Width, weight, or slant morphing between states (for video)

#### Color Approaches
- **Monochromatic**: Single hue, different saturation and brightness values — sophisticated and focused
- **Complementary contrast**: Opposite colors (blue/orange, purple/yellow) for maximum tension and energy
- **Analogous harmony**: Neighboring colors on the wheel for smooth, natural-feeling palettes
- **Neon on dark**: Electric vibrant colors against deep black or navy — nightlife energy, high contrast
- **Muted/Desaturated**: Sophisticated, editorial, timeless — colors with gray mixed in
- **Earth tones**: Warm browns, greens, terracotta, cream — grounded, natural, premium-casual
- **Pastel**: Soft, approachable, friendly — lower saturation, higher brightness
- **Black and white + one pop color**: Dramatic focus, cinematic, draws the eye exactly where you want
- **Brand-dominant**: Brand colors at full strength, reinforcing recognition — use sparingly, not for every post
- **Seasonal palette**: Adapt to the time of year (warm autumn tones, fresh spring greens, etc.)

#### Composition Techniques
- **Rule of thirds**: Classic, balanced, reliable — subject at intersection points
- **Center-dominant**: Bold, confrontational, symmetrical power — the subject demands attention
- **Asymmetric balance**: Dynamic tension, visual interest through deliberate imbalance
- **Generous negative space**: Breathing room, premium feel, letting one element shine
- **Bleed/Edge-to-edge**: Immersive, energetic, content fills every pixel
- **Frame within a frame**: Depth through doorways, windows, screens — creates voyeuristic intrigue
- **Diagonal flow**: Movement and energy, the eye follows the diagonal naturally
- **Grid layout**: Organized, clean, scannable — essential for carousel design
- **Overlapping elements**: Layered dimensionality, elements breaking out of their boundaries
- **Split composition**: Two halves contrasting (before/after, problem/solution, old/new)
- **Spiral/Golden ratio**: Natural, pleasing composition that feels "right" without knowing why

### 3. Platform-Specific Specs

Always design for the platform:

**Instagram:**
- Feed post: 1080x1080 (square) or 1080x1350 (portrait — recommended, takes up more screen space)
- Carousel: Up to 10 slides, each 1080x1350, maintain visual thread across slides
- Reel: 1080x1920, first frame must hook visually, safe zones for UI elements (top/bottom 15%)
- Story: 1080x1920, interactive element zones, avoid text in top/bottom 200px

**X/Twitter:**
- Image: 1200x675 (landscape) or 1200x1200 (square)
- Text must be readable at small thumbnail size in timeline
- High contrast is essential — X timeline is dense

**LinkedIn:**
- Image: 1200x627 or 1080x1080
- Document carousel: 1080x1080 or 1080x1350, PDF slides
- Professional but distinctive — stand out from the corporate gray

**Facebook:**
- Image: 1200x630 (landscape)
- Cover: 820x312
- Optimize for mobile viewing (most Facebook users are on mobile)

### 4. Creative Brief Template

For each post in the content calendar, include:

```
**Visual Direction:**
- **Concept:** {one-line creative vision — be bold and specific}
- **Style:** {aesthetic direction from vocabulary above — can combine 2-3}
- **Color Palette:** {specific colors — brand colors as base + context-appropriate accents}
- **Typography:** {font style, weight, treatment — how should text feel?}
- **Composition:** {layout approach and spatial arrangement}
- **Textures/Effects:** {overlays, filters, grain, special treatments}
- **Key Visual Element:** {what's the hero — product, person, text, graphic, illustration?}
- **Mood:** {emotional feel — one or two words: bold + urgent, calm + luxurious, playful + chaotic}
- **Platform Specs:** {dimensions and format-specific notes}
- **Reference Notes:** {any additional inspiration, references, or constraints}
```

## Creative Principles

1. **No two posts should look the same.** Vary composition, color weight, typography, and style across the calendar. A varied feed is an engaging feed.

2. **Brand consistency through color and voice, not templates.** The brand colors and tone should be a thread, not a cage. Express them differently each time.

3. **Hook the eye before the mind.** The visual hook is as important as the text hook. Someone must STOP scrolling before they READ your caption.

4. **Context-appropriate creativity.** An educational carousel needs different energy than a meme, a product launch, or a testimonial. Match the creative intensity to the content purpose.

5. **Scroll-stopping beats pretty.** Being distinctive beats being polished. A raw, bold, slightly imperfect design often outperforms a "nice" clean one. Perfection is boring.

6. **Study what competitors DON'T do visually.** Their visual gaps are your opportunity. If every competitor uses clean minimalism, go maximalist. If they all use stock photos, go illustration.

7. **Treat the carousel as a story.** Each slide should compel the viewer to swipe to the next. The first slide is the hook, the last slide is the CTA. The middle slides are the journey.

8. **Text hierarchy matters.** On any post with text overlay: one thing should be BIG (the hook), one thing should be medium (the supporting point), and everything else should be small or absent.
