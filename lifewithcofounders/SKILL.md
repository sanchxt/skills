# LifeWithCofounders

Sanchit (he, technical, deep into AI) and Sanchita (she), cofounders of Eravue, a service agency. They're on camera solo or together, in English.

**How the viewer should see them:** the AI-native founders who are always a step ahead. They break down the big news before you understand it, build things with AI that look impossible, and run their agency on it. People follow them to stay ahead. Business owners DM them because they've watched them build.

**Every video has to be big, modern and resourceful.** Small tips (a hidden phone setting, a minor feature) are not this account. When in doubt, ask whether the video makes them look ahead of the curve, *and* whether a stranger cares in the first second.

**Before writing, read** `../short-form-craft/SKILL.md` (the sibling skill folder) plus its `references/hooks.md`, `references/human-voice.md`, `references/retention.md` and `references/critic.md`. Read `log.md` here and lean toward what has worked.

## Lanes

**1. Big news, broken down for *you*.** Pick the tech, AI or cybersecurity story that's big right now, especially in India: breaches, government moves, major AI launches, a big company doing something wild. Use this structure:
- the scariest or most surprising true fact first;
- what actually happened, in one line a non-techie gets;
- what it means for the viewer;
- what they should do;
- the take, or the unanswered question, which becomes "part 2, follow so you don't miss it".

Verify every fact with a web search, and keep sources and dates in the notes for the user. Strong claims are fine as long as they're true. Speed matters, so it should be posted within 24–48 hours.

**2. "[Result] → here's how to build this."** Open on something impressive made with AI: an agent answering leads at 3 AM, an app built in an hour, an AI ad, a website from a napkin sketch, an automation that replaced a job. Then show how, in 3–5 fast steps with the actual tools on screen. End with "Comment [WORD] and I'll send you the workflow/prompt". Make sure the thing really gets sent. This is the main community and lead engine.

**3. Building the agency with AI (build in public).** Real things they've built for clients or for themselves, with real numbers: "we automated our whole proposal process", "this client's site took 2 days instead of 2 weeks". Result first. Business owners watching think "I want that", so about one in four of these can close with a soft pitch: "we build these for businesses, DM us". **These need real material.** Ask the user what they actually built or did, or leave `[REAL: …]` slots. Never invent clients, numbers or results.

**4. Takes.** Sharp opinions on AI, tech and the industry: "Most 'AI agencies' are just ChatGPT with a logo", "the job AI will kill first isn't the one you think". The opinion is said flatly, then proved, then defended. This lane is built for comments.

**5. Series.** Recurring formats that bring people back: "AI news you actually need this week", "We build it live", "Rate my client's website, then we rebuild it". Name the series on screen.

When asked for ideas, lead with lanes 1 and 2, and mix in 3–5. Every idea comes as a full script with hooks, unless the user asks for a quick list.

**This account owns builds that help a real business** (a site for a shop, an ad for a stall, "Fix It In An Hour"), because that's what brings in clients. Playful builds for laughs belong to sanchitwtf.

## Idea starters (for when there's no news)

These are shapes that reliably produce big, original ideas. Combine them with a specific tool, person or place:

- **AI clone experiment:** an AI version of one of them handles something real (internal chat, their calendar, a day of decisions), and the other reacts. *"For 48 hours my cofounder was texting an AI version of me."* Only clone themselves, only with consent, and never deceive clients or the public.
- **Rebuild for someone who benefits:** the rebuild concept works when the *outcome* matters to a normal person or a business owner, not when the tech is merely impressive. Examples: rebuild a viewer's business website from the comments, a local brand's terrible site, a small shop's ordering flow, then show the before/after and what it means for them (more orders, more bookings, looking legit). Avoid rebuilding big apps like IRCTC or Zomato just to show speed: devs care, clients don't.
- **AI vs a human, on real work:** our hiring test, a client brief, a design, a pitch. Blind judging, then the reveal.
- **Fix a real business in an hour:** a local shop, a chai tapri, a bad website. Before/after plus the owner's reaction. This is the most client-magnetic lane.
- **AI does a job for a day:** an intern's tasks, a designer's, theirs. What it nailed, and what it destroyed.
- **The scary-useful demo:** what an AI can find about you or your business from one photo, one number or one website, and how to protect yourself.
- **Build what the comments say:** take the top comment's idea, build it in a day, and post the result as a series.

Every idea still has to pass the idea-size test in short-form-craft and come with a clear send line.

**The audience check:** would a non-technical viewer care about the *outcome*? Would a small-business owner watching think "I want that for my business"? If only developers would be impressed, reframe the idea around who benefits, or drop it.

## Voice

- Fast, confident, a little excited. They sound like people who already tried the thing.
- Technical credibility comes from specifics: real tool names, real steps, real numbers. Explain jargon in half a sentence and never dumb down the cool part.
- **Two-person scenes:** they interrupt, ask the question the viewer is thinking ("wait, are we affected?"), and push back. No alternating paragraphs. No staged fights. Never imply they're a couple.
- **Avoid:** generic "cofounder banter" sketches, LinkedIn-speak, and hype without a demo.

## Output

Use the format from short-form-craft. Label speakers SANCHIT / SANCHITA, and put screen recordings, graphics and B-roll in brackets. Add cover text, a caption and the CTA. For news, put the sources and dates under the script for the user.

## Examples that set the bar

**Big news breakdown, about 55s** (real story, verified on 2026-11-04. Sources: The Federal 22 Oct, Business Today 21 Oct, Inc42 31 Oct 2026)
```
[0:00, face cam, green-screen: headline "eMudhra exits TLS business"]
SANCHIT: Google, Apple and Mozilla just blocked certificates from India's biggest digital-signature company. And the company's response was to shut that whole business down.
[0:06, graphic: browser padlock]
SANCHIT: Quick context. That padlock on every website? A company like eMudhra issues it. It's a certificate that says "this site is really who it claims to be."
[0:13, graphic: 8 certificates, "weak keys"]
SANCHIT: On October 17th, a researcher found eight of their certificates were made with weak keys. Weak enough that, in theory, someone could fake that padlock. A fake bank site with a real-looking lock.
[0:21] SANCHIT: Chrome blocked them in under two hours.
SANCHITA (off-screen): Okay, but are we affected?
[0:25, face cam]
SANCHIT: Probably not. MeitY says the digital signatures and eSign you use for tax and GST filing are safe, and no attack has been confirmed.
SANCHITA: Probably.
SANCHIT: Probably. Because these keys sat there for eighteen months. Through two audits. Nobody noticed.
[0:36, text: WHAT TO DO]
SANCHIT: So: update your browser. And if a site ever shows a certificate warning, never hit "proceed anyway." That button is how people get robbed.
[0:44] If you run a website, check who issued your SSL certificate. If it says eMudhra or emSign, replace it now.
[0:49] SANCHITA: Part two is the scary bit. So far, the only punishment has come from Google and Mozilla. Not from India.
TEXT: follow for part 2
```
Cover: `India's padlock problem`. Caption: `the keys were weak for 18 months. update your browser tonight.`
