# Instagram cold DM playbook

## What the research says (Nov 2026)

There is no independent study of Instagram cold DMs. Most numbers come from tool vendors, so treat them as direction, not fact.

| Finding | Source and caveat |
|---|---|
| **Shorter openers get more replies.** Accounts with 10%+ reply rates averaged **194 characters**; 3-10% averaged 329; under 3% averaged 481. Length was the only variable that separated the tiers. | Inflowave, 63,286 cold opener DMs from 100 accounts across 36 agencies (2024-26). Vendor data, observational. |
| Realistic reply rates: median account **~3%**; good accounts 10%+. One owner sent 1,100 DMs to business owners: **57 positive replies (~5%) → 11 calendar links → 6 calls**. | Inflowave; Skool self-report (unverified). Plan for ~3-8% replies and ~1-2% calls. |
| **Replies come fast:** 42% within 1 hour, 83% within 24 hours. Leads answered within 5 minutes kept talking far more often than those answered after a day. | Inflowave. → The founders must watch the inbox after a batch. |
| Cold DMs to non-followers land in **Requests**, unread until accepted. Instagram routes **templated, high-volume, link-first** messages to **Hidden Requests**, which don't notify. Biggest factor: no prior interaction. | Inrō (vendor). → No links in first DM, never identical text, human pace. |
| Following/engaging before a DM reportedly gives up to **4x** the reply rate. | Secondhand claim (Metros, probably X data). Unverified but cheap to test. |
| Wait ~**72 hours** before following up; following up inside 48h reads as pressure. A big share of replies come from follow-ups. | Metros (unsourced); consistent with email data. |
| Short messages at a **3rd-5th grade reading level** and **0-1 questions** get the most replies; 2+ questions lower replies. Best email length ~20-50 words. | Lavender, Hunter (34M emails), Reply.io. Email data, but the same reading behaviour applies to DMs. |
| Vague "quick question" openers underperform. A **specific, real observation** is what consistently earns replies. | SmartReach, Lemlist, Reply.io. |
| Voice notes reportedly reply 2-4x better, but only **after** a text exchange; cold voice notes as the first message don't fit. | Vendors (unverified). → Use voice notes in replies, not openers. |

## The rules that follow

1. **Short.** Aim for **120-220 characters**, never over 300. One or two sentences plus the ask. Count characters and show the count in the batch.
2. **Open with them, not you.** The first words are about their post, product, reel or comment section. Never "Hi, I'm Sanchit from Eravue, we are...".
3. **One true, specific observation** only that account could receive. If you can't name one, don't DM them.
4. **No pitch, offer a thing.** Don't describe Eravue's services. Offer something small and concrete they can say yes to: "want me to send it?", "want to see it?", "should I send the 1-min video?".
5. **One question at most**, easy to answer with "yes" or one word.
6. **No links, no prices, no emojis walls** in the first DM. One emoji max, only if their own captions use them.
7. **Every DM different.** Same structure is fine; identical text is not.
8. **Plain words.** Lowercase-casual is fine. Write like a busy founder typing on a phone. Light Hinglish only if their captions use it.
9. **Honest.** Every claim is something you saw. Every offered thing is something the founders can actually deliver within a day (see "Promises" below).
10. **Who you are:** sending from @eravuedotcom already says it's Eravue. If sending from a founder's personal account, add "(I run Eravue, a small web/AI studio in Delhi)".

## Two variants, always (A and B)

Every account in a batch gets **two** DMs unless the founders said otherwise for that run. Rules:

- **Different, not reworded.** A and B use different formulas from the list below, or the same formula with a different gap/offer (e.g. A: free mockup for the missing website, B: demo of instant replies for the missed enquiries). Both must stay true and specific to that account.
- **Both follow every rule above:** 120-230 characters, one question at most, no links, no prices, real observation, deliverable offer.
- **Label each** with its letter and formula (e.g. "B · question → demo"), plus its character count, so results can be compared.
- **Say what to have ready** for each variant (mockup, Loom, re-edit) in the batch entry.
- **Founders approve by letter** ("send 3A"). Log which variant was sent (tracker note and the Variant column in the founders' sheet).
- Within one batch, never use the same sentence in two accounts' DMs, and never send A and B to the same person.

## "Clickbait": what works and what backfires

Curiosity works when it's **true and you pay it off**. It backfires when it's vague, fake or looks like a scam.

**Use (honest curiosity):**
- "found 2 things on your site that are probably costing you enquiries. want me to send them?" (only if you found them and they're ready)
- "made a quick homepage mockup for [business]. want to see it?" (only if it exists or the founders will make it)
- "counted 6 'price?' comments on your last 3 reels with no reply." (a real count)
- "who edits your reels?" (real question; opens a conversation with creators)

**Never use:**
- Vague teasers: "quick question", "hey 👋", "can I ask you something?" (wastes the one message; feels like spam)
- Fake familiarity or flattery: "big fan!", "love your work!!" without saying what, "we met at..."
- Pretending to be a customer, a fan, or "just curious"
- Fake urgency or scarcity: "only 2 spots left", "offer ends tonight"
- Anything that looks like a Meta or security notice: "your account has an issue", "your page violated...", "verify your..." (gets reported as phishing)
- Bait like "I have a client for you" or "collab?" when it's a sales pitch
- Negative or shaming hooks: "your website is terrible", "your reels are flopping"

Rule of thumb: if the owner screenshots the DM and posts it, Eravue should look smart and helpful, not sneaky.

## Formulas

**A. Observation → gap → offer** (default)
> [specific thing about them]. noticed [gap, stated neutrally]. [I/we] [made/have] [small thing], want me to send it?

**B. Number hook** (best for unanswered comments)
> [count] people asked [price/details] on your last [n] [reels/posts], and [most/none] got a reply. [small thing that fixes it]. want a 1-min look?

**C. Sample first** (score 5 only; founders make the sample)
> made a quick [mockup / 20-sec re-edit / reply flow] for [business] using your [reel/menu/photos]. want me to send it over?

**D. Creator question** (video-editing / social-media)
> your [specific reel] was great, [specific detail]. who does your editing?
(If they say "me" → offer a free sample edit. If "an editor" → offer backup/extra volume or drop it.)

**E. Local peer** (honest: Eravue is in Delhi NCR)
> fellow [Delhi/Gurgaon] business here. [observation]. [offer]?

## Promises: what the founders must be able to deliver

Only offer what can be sent within a day of a reply. Note in the batch entry what's needed:
- **"quick fix idea" / "2 things I found"** → 3-5 bullet audit (Claude can write it from the audit notes).
- **"1-min video / demo"** → a Loom by the founders walking through the audit or a demo flow.
- **"mockup"** → a quick homepage mockup (Claude can generate an HTML mockup on request).
- **"free 20-sec re-edit"** → founders edit one of their reels.
- **"3 sample ads"** → AI creatives via MyContent.

## Follow-ups (Instagram: max 2, then stop)

Only if no reply. Check the thread first: if they replied, reacted or the request was declined, don't follow up. Each needs approval like a first DM.

- **Follow-up 1 (~day 3):** give a piece of the promised value without asking for anything.
  > here's the short version so you don't have to reply: [one concrete tip from the audit]. happy to send the rest if useful.
- **Follow-up 2 (~day 7):** easy exit.
  > no worries if now's not the time. if [the price-comment thing / the website] becomes a priority, just ping me here.

Then mark `no_reply`. (Tracker cadence after `--touch` is +3, +4, +7 days; stop after the 3rd touch on Instagram.)

## When they reply

Speed matters most here: replies answered within minutes keep going far more often.

1. **Thank + deliver** what was promised (links are fine now; the thread is accepted).
2. **One qualifying question** about their situation: "how do most enquiries come in right now, DM or WhatsApp?", "roughly how many orders a week?".
3. **Offer the next step:** a 15-min call or WhatsApp chat (+91 88605 49226) for "a clear plan and a fixed quote".
4. **Price asked early:** "depends on what you need; small jobs are fine. after a 15-min call I'll send a fixed quote." Never invent a number.
5. **Voice note** is good here if the founders want (reported to lift replies once talking).
6. **"Not interested" / "stop":** "totally fine, thanks for replying!" then `--status do_not_contact`.

Record replies: `python $S update L0012 --status replied --note "<what they said>"`.

## Testing what works

- Tag each DM's variant and formula in the tracker notes: `--note "variant:B question→demo"` on update. The founders' Drive sheet (Outreach Log, Variant column) compares A vs B reply rates.
- Run `/eravue-outreach review` weekly. At Eravue's volume (tens per week) results are directional; reaching statistical confidence needs hundreds per variant. Change one thing at a time, length first.
- Best times to send (unproven, test it): India owners often check DMs around lunch (12-2pm IST) and late evening (8-11pm IST). For US prospects, their evening.
