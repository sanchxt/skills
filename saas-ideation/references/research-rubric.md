# SaaS Ideation Research Rubric

Use this rubric to compare ideas consistently. Score 1-5 unless noted.

## Core Scores

- Usefulness: How painful, frequent, and expensive the problem is for a specific buyer.
- Speed to first revenue: How quickly a solo founder can get a paid user after a lean MVP.
- Recurring potential: Whether the product naturally supports monthly or annual subscription.
- Competition: 5 means competition validates demand but leaves reachable gaps; 1 means either no demand signal or impossible incumbents.
- Differentiation: How clearly the product can win with a narrow wedge, data source, workflow, niche, UX, automation, or distribution angle.
- Build complexity: 5 means buildable in days/weeks with AI and common APIs; 1 means infrastructure-heavy, regulated, or deep technical moat required.
- Platform fit: How well the idea fits web app, Chrome extension, Shopify app, Slack/Discord bot, mobile app, plugin, API, template/SaaS hybrid, or marketplace.
- Solo marketing ease: How directly one person can reach buyers through search, communities, cold outreach, partnerships, marketplace listings, short-form demos, or founder-led content.
- Upfront cost: 5 means negligible tooling/API spend before revenue; 1 means high data, ads, infra, licensing, or compliance costs.
- Risk: 5 means low platform, legal, data, support, and churn risk; 1 means fragile or high-risk.

## Weighted Decision

Use this default weighting for urgent recurring-revenue ideation:

| Factor | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Speed to first revenue | 20 |
| Usefulness | 15 |
| Solo marketing ease | 15 |
| Build complexity | 12 |
| Recurring potential | 12 |
| Upfront cost | 10 |
| Differentiation | 8 |
| Platform fit | 5 |
| Competition | 2 |
| Risk | 1 |

Normalize to a 100-point score. If exact math is overkill, use the weights qualitatively and explain the tradeoffs.

## Evidence Checklist

For finalist ideas, try to gather:

- At least 3 recent pain signals from communities, reviews, support forums, job posts, marketplace comments, or social/search demand.
- At least 3 direct competitors or substitutes, including pricing and positioning.
- A clear buyer segment with ability and habit of paying for software.
- One reachable acquisition channel the solo founder can start this week.
- One concrete MVP that removes a painful manual step or creates a measurable business result.
- One reason the idea can be sold before it is fully built.

## Fast-Revenue Bias

Prefer ideas with:

- B2B or prosumer buyers already spending money.
- Narrow niches where manual outreach can reach decision makers.
- Pain tied to revenue, compliance, time savings, lead generation, reporting, operations, or client deliverables.
- A "done-for-you first, SaaS later" path where the founder can charge for setup while productizing.
- Searchable demand or marketplace demand that does not require educating the market from zero.

Avoid ideas with:

- A broad consumer audience and no built-in distribution.
- Heavy dependence on paid ads, influencers, or app-store ranking.
- Expensive proprietary data or model training.
- Long enterprise procurement.
- Vague AI wrappers with no proprietary workflow insight.
- Outcomes that create legal, medical, or financial liability unless scoped as administrative support with clear disclaimers.

## Recommendation Labels

- Build now: Strong demand, reachable buyer, cheap MVP, credible first marketing channel.
- Validate first: Promising but needs proof of willingness to pay before building.
- Watchlist: Interesting trend but timing, complexity, or channel is not ready.
- Avoid: Weak payment intent, bad economics, saturated without a wedge, or too slow for the user's urgency.
