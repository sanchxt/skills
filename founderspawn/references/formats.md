# Minecraft Short Formats

Choose one primary format. A secondary device is allowed only when it sharpens the promise. Never force the same hook or beat sequence onto every mode.

## `brainrot-gif`

Aliases: `FounderSpawn-BrainrotGIF-Format`, `brainrot`, `gif commentary`.

Use Minecraft gameplay as the narrative spine and reaction inserts as punctuation. The GIFs/chips must react to a specific beat; they cannot substitute for a story.

1. Open on a Minecraft contradiction, loss, impossible result, or accusation.
2. Establish the rule or problem in one line.
3. Add two escalating proof beats, each with a new consequence.
4. Use at most one major reaction insert per beat; return to gameplay before the premise is forgotten.
5. Deliver a visible payoff, then one short choice/comment CTA or a clean loop.

Best length: 18–28 seconds. Keep meme references current only after browsing. Prefer owned/licensed reaction assets or original channel-made recurring chips.

## `story-over-gameplay`

Aliases: `FounderSpawn-Story-Telling`, `storytelling`, `parkour story`.

Use original gameplay as visual rhythm while the narration tells an original, permissioned, or user-supplied story. This format tests narrator personality more than Minecraft expertise, so use it selectively on a Minecraft-branded channel.

1. Start with the irreversible or socially awkward moment, not biography.
2. Name who wanted what.
3. Chain two or three turns through BUT/THEREFORE logic.
4. Reveal the decision, cost, or punchline.
5. End at the emotional peak; use a verdict question only if two reasonable sides exist.

Best length: 28–50 seconds. Do not scrape or lightly rewrite Reddit stories. Avoid unrelated recycled clips, generic confessions, and fake first-person claims.

## `in-game-story`

Aliases: `enacted story`, `Minecraft story`, `machinima`, `role-play`.

Tell the story through actions that can be recorded in Minecraft: a door moves, a villager disappears, a base changes, a friend sets a trap.

1. Show the consequence or impossible image.
2. Give the character a simple goal.
3. Make each Minecraft action cause the next problem.
4. Use a reveal that reinterprets an earlier visual.
5. Resolve the episode; tease a sequel only when one is actually planned.

Best length: 25–45 seconds. Label staged/role-play when a reasonable viewer could mistake it for a real SMP event.

## `horror`

Aliases: `horror story`, `ARG`, `Herobrine story`, `creepy Minecraft`.

Unease works better than a pile of loud monsters. Build around one anomaly with rules the viewer can track.

1. Open on evidence that should not exist.
2. Establish one normal expectation.
3. Break that expectation twice, more specifically each time.
4. Reveal a consequence or partial explanation.
5. End on one altered detail that rewards rewatching.

Best length: 25–50 seconds. Clearly identify mods, fiction, or reenactment outside the narration where appropriate. Do not claim a fake seed, sighting, or leaked feature is real.

## `quiz`

Aliases: `guess the block`, `trivia challenge`, `three clues`.

Participation begins immediately; no generic intro.

1. State the exact task and answer space.
2. Give three clues from broad to discriminating.
3. Use a short visual countdown or lock-in beat.
4. Reveal the answer with proof.
5. Ask for the viewer's clue number, score, or next category—not all three.

Best length: 15–24 seconds. Each clue must point to only one defensible answer by the reveal. Avoid obscure wording that turns the quiz into luck.

## `myth-test`

Aliases: `experiment`, `comment test`, `does this work`.

1. Show the strangest result or last test.
2. State the claim and what would prove it.
3. Run a control or baseline.
4. Escalate one variable.
5. Reveal the verified result and invite one next myth or bounded choice.

Best length: 20–35 seconds. Keep commands, mods, edition, and version visible when they affect the outcome.

## `challenge-clutch`

Aliases: `Minecraft but`, `one heart`, `speedrun`, `item challenge`.

1. Open inside the danger with the rule already visible.
2. State the single restriction.
3. Show two meaningful progress jumps, not travel filler.
4. Introduce a late obstacle caused by the rule.
5. Resolve success or failure and stop near the peak.

Best length: 20–38 seconds. The restriction must create decisions, not just decorate ordinary gameplay.

## `fact-or-rant`

Aliases: `trivia`, `rare fact`, `Minecraft opinion`, `history`.

1. Open with a specific claim, correction, or contradiction.
2. Show the object or old/new comparison.
3. Explain the reason or history in one causal chain.
4. Add one caveat, exception, or implication.
5. End with a defensible opinion or related question.

Best length: 18–30 seconds. Browse for version history, rarity, removed features, and current changes. Prefer "one of the rarest" over unsupported absolutes.

## `mistake-fix`

Aliases: `tip`, `tutorial`, `hack`, `useful Minecraft`.

1. Show the loss, slow method, or bad result.
2. Name the exact mistake.
3. Demonstrate the corrected action.
4. Compare outcomes visibly.
5. Ask which mistake or tool to test next.

Best length: 18–32 seconds. The fix must save time, resources, risk, or confusion; avoid fake "secret" tips.

## `ranking-comparison`

Aliases: `ranking`, `versus`, `best item`.

1. State one judging rule.
2. Test three to five candidates with comparable evidence.
3. Make the order change or narrow as proof arrives.
4. Reveal a defensible but debatable winner.
5. Ask what was robbed or let comments choose the next test.

Best length: 22–38 seconds. Use owned footage and original analysis. Do not compile other creators' clips with shallow narration.

## `build-transformation`

Aliases: `build`, `before after`, `timelapse`, `design`.

1. Flash the finished result or functional surprise.
2. Show the ugly constraint or empty start.
3. Use three large, legible progress beats.
4. Reveal a feature that changes how the build works or feels.
5. Ask viewers to choose one next upgrade.

Best length: 18–35 seconds. Do not spend narration listing every block; explain the design problem and satisfying decisions.

## `update-news`

Aliases: `snapshot`, `new feature`, `patch`.

1. Show the most visible verified change.
2. State what changed and the edition/version/date.
3. Demonstrate why it matters.
4. Give one benefit, limitation, or unresolved question.
5. Ask what verified feature to test next.

Best length: 20–35 seconds. Use official Minecraft sources first. Never manufacture urgency.

## `subscriber-comment`

Aliases: `testing your comments`, `comment reply`.

1. Show the exact comment sticker immediately.
2. Translate it into a fair test or build goal.
3. Run visible controls or progress beats.
4. Resolve it honestly.
5. Invite one narrow category of next comments.

Best length: 20–35 seconds. This is a community wrapper; pair it with myth, build, challenge, or quiz mechanics.

## Selection Guidance

For a young channel, favor formats that build a Minecraft-specific reason to return: `myth-test`, `quiz`, `in-game-story`, `horror`, `mistake-fix`, and a more original `brainrot-gif` series. Treat `story-over-gameplay` as an intentional personality experiment and generic clip rankings as a rights/originality risk, not default growth engines.
