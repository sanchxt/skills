# Hook And Script Engine

Use this for every finished script. For non-Minecraft stories, apply the object, character, and stakes of that story; Minecraft is only the optional visual medium. Read `story-and-brainrot.md` for expanded narrative modes. The goal is not maximum loudness; it is maximum clarity plus an unresolved, honest question.

## Engineer The First Two Seconds

The hook is a coordinated package:

- frame zero: result, danger, anomaly, object, or choice for enacted scenes; for background gameplay, hook text or a subject insert can carry the premise;
- first caption: usually 2–5 concrete words;
- first spoken clause: usually 4–7 words;
- complete premise: normally clear within 8–12 spoken words.

Useful hook engines:

| Engine | Use when | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction | Two true-looking ideas collide | "This is the safest ___, but ___." |
| Consequence first | A mistake or decision has a cost | "One block deleted my entire ___." |
| Impossible proof | The visual appears to violate expectations | "This house moves every night." |
| Rule + stakes | A challenge has one legible restriction | "I get one bucket to survive ___." |
| Correction | The viewer likely uses something wrong | "You're wasting ___ every time you ___." |
| Participation | The viewer can play immediately | "Name this block before clue three." |
| Unease | One detail is wrong and trackable | "That door was not there yesterday." |

Contradiction is powerful only when both halves are specific and the video can reconcile them. Do not staple "but" onto an ordinary fact.

Reject hooks that:

- need channel lore, a title, or several pronouns to understand;
- announce the topic instead of opening tension;
- use empty intensity: "insane," "crazy," "most dangerous," or "you won't believe" without proof;
- hide basic context and call it curiosity;
- promise a result the footage cannot show.

## Build Retention With Causality

Use one main open loop for a single story. Each beat should answer one small question while creating the next. Shower-thought anthologies may instead deliver several distinct question/payoff units under one clear theme.

Prefer:

> goal → obstacle → decision → worse obstacle or proof → payoff

For explanatory formats:

> surprising claim → visible example → reason → exception/implication → payoff

For quizzes:

> task → clue → narrower clue → lock-in → answer/proof

Use BUT/THEREFORE as a diagnostic, not repetitive spoken connectors. If two beats only connect with "and then," one is probably filler. A change in zoom, caption, GIF, or sound does not rescue a beat that adds no meaning.

Do not hold all value until the end. Give useful proof or progress every few seconds, then deliver the strongest resolution near the end.

## Make It Sound Human

Write how an amused player would tell one friend what happened.

- Use contractions and ordinary words.
- Mix short impact lines with one slightly longer explanatory line.
- Give reactions a cause: "Okay, that should not work" is useful after visible proof; random "bro" is not.
- Prefer exact nouns over pronouns: creeper, trapdoor, powder snow, Warden.
- Let personality come from judgment, surprise, restraint, and specificity—not a pile of slang.
- Use at most one small self-correction or aside when it sounds earned.
- Keep one idea per sentence unless a deliberate contradiction needs both halves.

Remove AI-sounding filler such as:

- "But here's the twist"
- "What happens next is insane"
- "Little did I know"
- "In the world of Minecraft"
- "Let's dive in"
- "This changes everything"
- "You won't believe"
- repeated "but then" or "and then"

Do not fabricate first-person experience. If the footage is hypothetical, write it as a challenge, demonstration, or fictional scene.

## Close Without Killing Retention

Resolve the promise before asking for anything. Choose one ending:

- Result: stop on the visible answer or emotional peak.
- Loop: the final image or phrase changes how the opening is understood.
- Comment: ask a bounded story choice, hypothetical, score, prediction, or defensible verdict.
- Subscribe: name the exact repeatable future value or genuinely planned next test.
- Watch next: point to a directly related video that is actually attached with YouTube's Related Video feature.

Keep a spoken CTA to one short sentence. Never stack like, comment, share, and subscribe. Never use unrelated moral-pressure bait.

## Private Quality Gate

Revise until all are true:

1. A cold viewer encounters immediate tension and can understand the premise promptly; do not mutilate a natural hook to meet a rigid two-second cutoff.
2. The spoken hook matches the caption and any subject insert; enacted footage depicts it, while background footage remains compatible and readable.
3. Every sentence changes knowledge, stakes, or emotion.
4. Pronouns have obvious referents.
5. The payoff answers the exact opening question.
6. The CTA, if present, asks for one relevant action after value.
7. The script contains no unsupported fact, fake event, or unusable shot.
8. Reading it aloud sounds like speech, not a caption essay or advertisement.

If the script still feels generic, change the premise, consequence, or proof—not merely the adjectives.
