# Research and sourcing

Use this reference whenever the output depends on facts, quotations, interviews, episode canon, scene interpretation, or current online material.

## Real-person route

Search broadly enough to discover the story, then narrow to the strongest evidence:

1. Direct interview, speech, memoir, verified account, official sports/award/organization record, or contemporaneous source.
2. Original reporting and reputable profiles with named authors and dates.
3. High-quality documentary or secondary source that identifies its evidence.
4. Wikipedia, fan pages, Reddit, existing Shorts, and unsourced videos only as leads.

For a comeback or transformation, establish:

- the precise “before” condition;
- what was lost or at risk;
- the documented low point without exaggeration;
- the actual pivot and the subject's agency in it;
- the visible outcome;
- what the subject said about the experience, if available;
- the dates needed to prevent a misleading compression.

Build a claim ledger:

| Candidate line/claim | Best source | Exact support | Date | Confidence | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use `high` confidence for direct/official support, `medium` for well-sourced inference or consistent reputable reporting, and `low` for disputed/unclear claims. Do not script low-confidence claims as fact.

### Sensitive claims

For abuse, discrimination, body shaming, injury, health, addiction, crime, relationships, money, or private motives:

- attribute personal accounts (“she later said…”) rather than converting them into omniscient fact;
- preserve the speaker, date, and context;
- seek corroboration where appropriate;
- distinguish allegation, opinion, diagnosis, and documented event;
- never invent an internal thought to make a line more emotional.

## Fictional-canon route

Research the actual work before fan interpretation. Establish:

- season, episode number, episode title, and chronology;
- what the scene visibly shows;
- exact dialogue when needed;
- earlier behavior that makes the change legible;
- later behavior that proves whether it lasted;
- official creator/cast interviews if they add intent;
- whether a proposed reading is canon, a reasonable interpretation, or fan theory.

Use honest phrasing:

- Canon: “In ‘Forks,’ Richie…”
- Interpretation: “The episode frames the suit as…”
- Inference: “A strong reading is…”
- Fan theory: label it explicitly and do not present it as the show's intent.

Do not flatten a season-long arc into a false single cause. It is acceptable to say a scene crystallizes or reveals a change rather than causing all of it.

## Interview and YouTube research

Prefer the subject's, publisher's, studio's, broadcaster's, league's, or festival's official upload. Verify in the actual video:

- exact URL and title;
- publisher/channel identity;
- upload date;
- in/out timestamp;
- exact short quotation or action;
- whether captions/transcripts match the audio;
- surrounding context that changes the meaning.

If the exact timestamp cannot be verified, say so and provide search terms instead of inventing precision. Never recommend downloading a YouTube video in violation of its terms; offer an official embed/remix/licensing/permission path or a voiceover fallback.

## Existing viral videos and community research

Use successful Shorts/Reels/TikToks to identify hypotheses about:

- subject framing;
- opening image and line;
- duration and beat density;
- audience vocabulary;
- what viewers send, debate, misunderstand, or request next.

Do not copy scripts, distinctive lines, shot sequences, or unlicensed compilations. Reddit reports are anecdotal. Cite them as community observations and test them against the user's own analytics.

## Source delivery

Provide a compact source list with descriptive Markdown links. Put sources next to disputed or high-stakes notes when clarity benefits. Separate:

- verified facts used in narration;
- useful background omitted from the script;
- clip sources;
- unresolved claims or conflicting accounts.

Include an access date for unstable sources, platform libraries, and live trend pages.
