# Analytics and iteration

Use when the user supplies analytics, asks why a post underperformed, or wants a testing/growth plan.

## Analyze three layers separately

| Layer | Signals | Likely issue when weak |
|---|---|---|
| Stop | YouTube stayed-to-watch; earliest TikTok/Instagram retention checkpoint; first-second curve | subject framing, first frame, first line, premise clarity |
| Stay | engaged views, AVD, APV, completion, curve shape, rewatches | excess setup, broken causality, repeated information, delayed proof |
| Act | sends/shares, likes, comments, saves, profile visits, follows/subscribers per viewer | payoff, social value, account promise, CTA fit |

YouTube frames recommendation performance as appeal, engagement, and satisfaction. Instagram leadership has emphasized watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach. TikTok says user interactions—including watching in full, skipping, and time spent—usually outweigh weak settings signals.

## Do not invent universal thresholds

A completion rate is inseparable from video length, audience, and topic. Compare each platform and duration band to the account's own rolling median (for example, the last 10 reasonably comparable posts).

Normalize action metrics:

- shares/sends ÷ reach or engaged viewers;
- likes ÷ reach;
- comments ÷ reach;
- follows/subscribers gained ÷ 1,000 engaged viewers;
- profile visits ÷ reach.

After YouTube view-count changes, prefer engaged views, stayed-to-watch, AVD, and APV for historical comparisons over raw public views.

## Curve diagnosis

- Immediate cliff: weak/unclear first frame or promise, unknown subject without universal stakes.
- Drop immediately after hook: repeated premise or delayed start.
- Middle sag: no consequence, proof, contrast, or new question.
- Drop before payoff: tease overstayed its welcome or an unnecessary fact interrupted momentum.
- Drop during CTA: CTA too long, too early, generic, or unrelated to account promise.
- Spike: could be delight, sharing, or confusion; inspect the exact moment before calling it positive.

## Test one meaningful variable

Useful controlled tests:

- hook framing while keeping body/payoff substantially stable;
- opening visual while keeping the first line stable;
- 25–30 versus 40–50 second versions of the same factual spine;
- voiceover-open versus authentic-clip-open;
- emotional reframe versus genuine interpretive-question ending;
- subject lane: sports, entertainment-career, fictional transformation.

Do not change hook, duration, subject, song, ending, and posting time and then attribute the result to one factor.

## Winner labels

- Hook winner: stop metric above the comparable baseline.
- Story winner: AVD/APV/completion above baseline after a normal stop rate.
- Share winner: sends/shares per reach above baseline.
- Conversion winner: follows/subscribers per engaged viewer above baseline.

Reuse the winning principle, not the exact script, visuals, or template. Originality and audience trust compound; interchangeable mass production does not.

## Platform notes

- YouTube says publish time is not known to affect long-term performance. Do not overdiagnose timing.
- Hashtags and metadata help context/search but do not rescue rejection.
- Do not declare a post dead from an alleged two-hour “test batch”; no universal staged-distribution formula is public.
- Reels Trial can test alternate subjects/hooks with non-followers when available.
- Qualitative comments reveal confusion, emotional resonance, and demand for the next subject; treat them as research, not just a count.

## Revision output

When revising from analytics, provide:

1. evidence-backed diagnosis ranked by likely impact;
2. the earliest retention failure and what caused it;
3. one revised hook/first frame;
4. one tighter body change;
5. one payoff/CTA change if action metrics were weak;
6. a single next-post hypothesis and the metric that would falsify it.
