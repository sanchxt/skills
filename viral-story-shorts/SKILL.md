# Viral Story Shorts

Create an accurate, emotionally strong short that earns three decisions: stop, stay, and act. Optimize the odds of viewer satisfaction; never promise virality or invent a universal retention threshold.

## Establish the brief

Use supplied constraints. If unspecified, default to:

- one self-contained 35–55 second story;
- a cold general audience that may know the face/show but not the backstory;
- cinematic voiceover with selective authentic dialogue;
- a clean 9:16 master adapted to TikTok, Reels, and Shorts;
- one natural CTA after the payoff;
- a plain ElevenLabs-ready narration block plus cited production notes.

Ask a question only when a missing choice would materially change the story. Otherwise state the useful assumption and proceed. Respect song options, target duration, tone, platform, supplied footage, language, and TTS model.

## Research before writing

Browse current sources for every factual subject, episode interpretation, interview/scene recommendation, song trend, platform rule, or clip URL. Treat YouTube, Reddit, fan pages, wikis, and existing Shorts as leads or audience evidence, not sufficient support for sensitive factual claims.

Classify the subject:

- For a real person or event, read [references/research-and-sourcing.md](references/research-and-sourcing.md).
- For a fictional character/show, use the fictional-canon route in that same reference.
- When clips, songs, timestamps, edit direction, or rights matter, read [references/production-and-music.md](references/production-and-music.md).
- When the user requests analytics diagnosis, growth testing, or a revision based on performance, read [references/analytics-and-iteration.md](references/analytics-and-iteration.md).

Build a small claim ledger before drafting. Resolve contradictions, attribute sensitive statements, separate fact from inference, and omit claims that cannot be supported. Prefer direct interviews, official records, creator/show sources, and original reporting. Link every material source in the delivered research notes.

## Select one story spine

Do not summarize an entire life, career, season, or show. Select one transformation with:

1. recognizable subject or universally understandable conflict;
2. concrete stakes;
3. a decision/opportunity that changes the direction;
4. visible proof of change;
5. a final meaning that reinterprets the familiar person or scene.

Use **Promise → Pressure → Pivot → Proof → Meaning → Next**. Read [references/story-and-script.md](references/story-and-script.md) for hook design, causal beats, pacing, word budgets, and endings.

## Draft for retention and satisfaction

Write the hook and final line first. The first frame and first spoken line must jointly establish recognition, tension, and a truthful destination. Start the action immediately; omit greetings, throat-clearing, channel branding, and premature CTAs.

Connect the body through cause and consequence—often “but” and “therefore”—instead of an “and then” chronology. Each line must change the situation, increase pressure, reveal proof, or deepen meaning. Re-hook only when a prior question is partially answered. Do not confuse random cuts, excessive captions, or misleading withholding with suspense.

Pay off the exact opening promise. After the proof, add one concise human interpretation and at most one natural action. Optimize for sends/shares through awe, recognition, hidden context, identity, or genuine debate; never use deliberate mistakes, coercive engagement bait, fabricated hostility, or humiliation solely for comments.

## Deliver cleanly

Unless the user asks for only one component, provide:

1. **Angle and evidence:** one-sentence premise, factual spine, uncertainty notes, and linked sources.
2. **Hook set:** three materially different truthful hooks; label the recommended one and why it should stop the intended viewer.
3. **ElevenLabs-ready voiceover:** one fenced block containing narration only. Exclude citations, headings, timestamps, editor notes, quotation marks used as directions, and bracketed stage directions. Add supported TTS/audio tags only when the user requests them or specifies a compatible model.
4. **Timing check:** word count, estimated natural duration, and target range.
5. **Production notes:** interview/scene clip options with verified URLs and timestamps, a voiceover fallback, caption emphasis, and music arc when relevant.
6. **Packaging:** platform-native title/caption/cover text and one CTA when requested.
7. **Sources and rights notes:** distinguish verified availability from suggestions the editor must check.

If a full breakdown is requested, use the schema in [references/deliverable-templates.md](references/deliverable-templates.md). Align timecodes to the actual narration budget, not arbitrary five-second blocks.

## Non-negotiable guardrails

- Never fabricate a quote, timestamp, URL, lyric, episode event, motive, award, score, date, diagnosis, or private thought.
- Quote interviews sparingly and preserve meaning. Identify paraphrases as paraphrases.
- Do not impersonate the subject with an AI-cloned voice or imply endorsement.
- Do not claim that credit, short duration, cropping, pitch/speed changes, or “no copyright intended” clears third-party footage or music.
- Prefer licensed, permissioned, public-domain, official press, in-product remix, or original assets. Provide a rights-safe fallback.
- Keep narration and interpretation substantially original; scraped clips plus template captions are not enough.
- For sensitive experiences, preserve the subject's language and dignity. Do not turn abuse, body shaming, illness, addiction, death, or trauma into unsupported shock copy.
- Make fictional interpretations defensible from canon and label inference or fan theory.
- Keep source links outside the pasteable voiceover block.
