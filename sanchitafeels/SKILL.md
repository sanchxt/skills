# SanchitaFeels

Sanchita's account: life, relationships, the little things people do when they love or miss someone, everyday psychology, and calm aesthetic videos. The vibe is chill, but the *hooks are not*. On Explore, a soft opener is invisible. Each video needs a first line that makes someone stop, feel caught, and send it to the person it's about.

**Before writing, read** `../short-form-craft/SKILL.md` (the sibling skill folder) plus its `references/hooks.md`, `references/human-voice.md` and `references/critic.md`. Read `log.md` here and lean toward what has worked.

## Explore-grade hooks

The first line has to work like a headline and a punch at once. Patterns that stop people in this niche:

- **A bold claim about love or people:** "Nobody in India says 'I love you'. We say 'reached?'"
- **A rule:** "If he says he's bad at texting, believe him."
- **A callout:** "The friend who always says 'on my way' is still in the shower."
- **A truth that stings a little:** "Your 3 AM thoughts aren't facts. They're just loud."
- **A generational or cultural specific:** Gen Z habits, situationships, internet life, and sometimes Indian families. Specific enough that the right people feel seen.
- **A psychology fact with a jolt:** a real study told as a story, with a surprising number.

Avoid soft starters ("There's a specific kind of…", "Sometimes…", "You know when…") unless the next three words are electric.

Every video should have an obvious *send target*: "send this to your 'reached?' person", or the friend, the ex, the mom, the group chat.

## Voice

A warm, sharp friend with opinions. She's funny about feelings, bold in her first line, and tender in her last. Specific behaviours, not named emotions. Never a therapist, guru or wellness poster. Banned: healing journey, protect your peace, your feelings are valid, you are enough, main character energy, hits different, "and that's okay".

She says "I" only about things the user confirms really happened. Otherwise observe ("some dads…", "the friend who…").

## Formats

1. **Text-on-screen reel (6–12s).** One or two bold lines over calm footage of her or her life, with a song from the platform library. These are the most-sent format, so make lots of them.
2. **Voiceover list or story (20–40s).** A bold claim, 4–6 rapid specific examples, and a last line that flips it into a feeling. "Reached?" below is the template.
3. **Psychology with a jolt (30–45s).** A real study told as a story, with the surprising number and what it means for you. Verify the facts and keep the source in the notes for the user.
4. **Rules and takes (15–30s).** Relationship or friendship rules said with confidence, built for comments ("agree or not?").
5. **Internet theories (20–35s).** Red string, orange peel, burnt toast. Give her take quickly, label it a theory, no lecture.

Mix roughly: lots of text reels, plus two or three bolder voiceover or talking pieces a week.

## Variety

Don't let the account become "Indian relatable" only. Most videos should land for anyone, anywhere. Indian-specific pieces are seasoning, about one in four at most. Rotate across:
- **love and situationships:** texting, exes, almost-relationships, being the one who cares more;
- **friendship:** drifting apart, the group chat, the friend who always plans, adult friendships;
- **self:** overthinking, being alone vs. lonely, growing up, your twenties, comparison, starting over;
- **internet life:** reels as love language, screen time, posting vs. living, read receipts;
- **work and ambition:** burnout, Sunday-night dread, quitting, being "good at hiding it";
- **psychology and theories:** real studies, plus red string, orange peel and burnt toast;
- **calm moments:** rain, late-night drives, 5 AM thoughts, small joys.

In any batch, no two ideas should share the same topic area, format or emotional note.

## Care

Don't diagnose people ("he's a narcissist") and don't give therapy instructions. For self-harm, abuse or crisis topics, keep it gentle and add a current Indian helpline in the caption.

## Output

Use the format from short-form-craft. Text reels: the exact on-screen text with line breaks, the footage, the song mood, the caption (with a send target), and the cover. Add a one-line YouTube title for Shorts.

## Examples that set the bar

**Voiceover list, about 25s.** Hook text: `indian love language`
```
Nobody in India says "I love you."
We say "reached?"
"Eat something before you leave."
"Take a jacket, it's cold." It's twenty-four degrees.
"Why didn't you call?"
"I kept the last piece for you."
Some dads have never once said I love you.
They've asked "reached?" about four thousand times.
```
Caption: `send this to your "reached?" person`

**Text reels, about 7s each**
```
if he says he's bad at texting, believe him.
just don't wait for him to get better at it.
```
```
your 3am thoughts aren't facts.
they're just loud.
```

**Psychology, about 35s** (Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, 2000, the "spotlight effect")
```
Scientists made students walk into a room wearing the most embarrassing t-shirt they could find.
Then asked: how many people noticed?
They guessed half.
It was less than a quarter.
That's the spotlight effect. You're sure everyone clocked the voice crack, the outfit, the text that was a bit too long.
They didn't.
They were too busy worrying about their own shirt.
```
