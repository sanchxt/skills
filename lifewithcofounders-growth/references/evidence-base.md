# Evidence base

Research refreshed: 2026-09-06. Re-check current claims before using them operationally. This file supports platform and testing principles; it does not establish the account's permanent niche.

## Platform-supported or directly documented

- Instagram's in-app Best Practices hub provides general and personalized guidance across creation, engagement, reach, monetization, and guidelines, including posting frequency, attention, Reel length, trending audio, hashtags, and the relationship between Reels and follower growth. Account-specific guidance should outrank generic benchmarks when accessible.
- Trial Reels are designed to show a Reel to non-followers first and expose early performance, making them useful for testing unfamiliar genres, topics, and storytelling formats. Meta's published cohort results are encouraging but explicitly not a guarantee for an individual account.
- Meta says messaging is Instagram's most popular way to share photos and videos and that Reels are reshared billions of times daily across Meta platforms. This supports designing for genuine private sending rather than bolted-on “share this” copy.
- Meta's Edits documentation identifies skip rate as a factor that can affect distribution. This supports diagnosing the opening without inventing a universal hook length or skip-rate threshold.
- Meta's 2026 company update says Instagram increased the prevalence of original content in US recommendations during Q4 2025. Separate 2026 Facebook guidance describes direct creation and substantive transformation as original, while low-value reposting or narration is deprioritized. The Facebook-specific enforcement details should not be presented as an exact Instagram policy, but both sources favor meaningful original contribution over compilation-style posting.
- YouTube's official Shorts discussion with product lead Todd Sherman and creator Jenny Hoyos emphasizes a first-second hook and concise story “bits.” This is useful cross-platform creative guidance, not proof of a fixed Instagram rule.

## India-specific context

Meta's June 2026 India release reports findings from Meta-commissioned Ipsos studies with more than 4,000 respondents across 23 cities, including metro, tier 2/3, and rural areas. It identifies beauty/makeup, fashion/trends, lifestyle, fitness/wellness, comedy/humor, sports, and travel among highly engaged Reels genres in the survey.

Use this as directional evidence that a broad India-focused creator account may reasonably test mainstream lifestyle, comedy, sports, travel, and cultural content. Do not interpret a platform-commissioned category survey as proof that any specific LifeWithCofounders concept will perform, or as a mandate to cover every listed genre.

## Directional principles

- Human-led original footage provides more opportunities for personality, recognition, and transformation than faceless corporate posts, but faces alone do not make an idea strong.
- A repeatable format can reduce production friction and help viewers recognize a creator, but the account should not copy another creator's wording, identity, jokes, or footage.
- Strong openings, visible progression, payoff, and audience relevance are more useful creative variables than a universal duration or posting-frequency rule.
- Reels and carousels can serve different jobs; broad third-party studies vary by sample and metric, so use the account's own comparable results.
- Current events can provide discovery, but a factually correct news recap is not automatically entertaining or follow-worthy.
- A consistent pair of creators can become the recognition device while subjects remain broad. This is a hypothesis to test, not a platform rule.

## What the evidence does not establish

The available sources do **not** establish that:

- the account should be primarily about cofounders, clients, startups, or work;
- the creators should present themselves as a couple;
- question-and-answer explainers caused another account's growth;
- one or two posts per day is universally optimal;
- using a trending audio will improve distribution;
- one viral post proves a durable niche;
- generic “identity fit” matters more than cold-viewer interest;
- a high self-assigned idea score predicts performance.

Account positioning should therefore remain provisional until enough LifeWithCofounders posts and Insights can be compared.
