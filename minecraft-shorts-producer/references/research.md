# Research And Verification

Browse when an idea or package depends on current information. Use primary sources for factual claims whenever possible.

## Browse For

- current Minecraft releases, snapshots, previews, events, commands, and edition differences
- current YouTube Shorts product behavior, analytics definitions, monetization, or policies
- trending Minecraft topics, competitor patterns, and platform-native sounds
- exact rarity rates, loot tables, mechanics, records, or version history that may have changed

## Source Priority

1. Official Minecraft/Mojang patch notes, release notes, help, and verified announcements
2. Official YouTube Help, Creator Insider, YouTube Creators, and YouTube Blog
3. Minecraft Wiki pages when primary documentation is insufficient; verify edition and version
4. Direct creator videos or interviews for observable format patterns
5. Reddit and creator communities for hypotheses, pain points, and anecdotes—not algorithm facts

## Evidence Rules

- Cite date-sensitive recommendations and distinguish verified facts from inference.
- If a mechanic differs between Java and Bedrock, specify which version the video should record.
- If a claim cannot be verified, turn it into an honest on-camera experiment instead of asserting it.
- Never fabricate a patch, probability, seed, world record, or "before it gets patched" deadline.
- Do not recommend copyrighted trend audio as safe for monetization without checking its usage context. Prefer YouTube Audio Library, platform-native audio, original SFX, or a described sound mode.

## Competitor Research

Study the underlying promise, first frame, pacing, proof, series identity, CTA, and subscriber path. Do not copy exact scripts, branding, thumbnails, or staged events. Look for gaps such as weak payoff, interchangeable narration, untested claims, or missing community participation.
