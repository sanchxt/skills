# ElevenLabs Narration

The final script must sound like a fast, amused Minecraft player speaking to a friend, not a documentary narrator or ad.

## Copy-Ready Block

Place the complete narration in one plain text code block. Do not insert timestamps, emotion tags, shot directions, headings, bracketed notes, or on-screen captions inside it. Put all performance direction outside the block.

## Length And Pace

- 15-22 seconds: roughly 45-65 words
- 23-35 seconds: roughly 70-100 words
- 36-60 seconds: roughly 110-160 words, only for a real story or multi-stage test

Treat word counts as pacing guides, not padding targets. Cut repeated setup before adding speed.

## Writing Style

- Put a hard, understandable hook in the first 8-12 words.
- Use short sentences and concrete Minecraft nouns.
- Use commas for tiny pauses and periods for beat changes.
- Use an ellipsis once at most, only when a real reveal follows.
- Spell out awkward numbers or symbols when it improves pronunciation.
- Use one small human beat such as "okay, wait," "I thought this was fake," or "that was way too close."
- Avoid all-caps, tongue twisters, forced slang, repeated rhetorical questions, and empty superlatives.

## CTA Rules

The CTA comes after the promised payoff and asks for one action.

Prefer:

- a bounded choice: "Comment water, slime, or cobweb."
- the next test: "Tell me which Warden myth to test next."
- a genuine sequel: "Subscribe, because tomorrow I'm testing this from world height."
- a value promise: "Follow for the next survival mistake."

Do not request likes, comments, and subscriptions together. Do not use "part two" when no part two is planned.

## TTS Direction Outside The Script

Include:

- voice mood
- approximate pace
- where to slow down
- the key word to emphasize
- one intentional pause before the reveal, if useful
