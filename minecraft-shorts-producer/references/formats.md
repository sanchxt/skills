# Minecraft Short Formats

Choose one primary format per Short. A secondary device such as a ranking inside a challenge is acceptable only when it makes the promise clearer.

## Myth Or Experiment

Best for subscriber comments, obscure mechanics, fall-damage tests, mob behavior, redstone, and item interactions.

1. Show the strangest result or final test.
2. State the claim and stakes.
3. Run two quick proof beats or controls.
4. Reveal the verified result.
5. Ask for the next concrete myth or offer choices.

## Danger Or Clutch

Best for hardcore, one-heart survival, lava, void, Warden, Nether, and last-second saves.

1. Open inside the danger.
2. Rewind only as much as needed to explain the rule.
3. Remove obvious escape options.
4. Attempt one decisive clutch.
5. Resolve survival or failure, then name the next harder test.

## Mistake And Fix

Best for diamonds, enchanting, inventory, farms, villagers, building, exploration, and beginner traps.

1. Show the loss or bad result.
2. Identify one specific mistake.
3. Show the corrected action.
4. Compare the outcomes visually.
5. Ask which mistake to test next.

## Rare Fact, Item, Or Mechanic

Best for rare loot, removed items, unusual blocks, structures, mobs, and version history.

1. Show the object or proof immediately.
2. Ask why it is unusual.
3. Explain one route, condition, or historical reason.
4. Show a second proof or caveat.
5. End on a loopable close-up or next rarity.

Verify rarity claims and version differences. Prefer "one of the rarest" over an unsupported absolute.

## Comparison Or Ranking

Best for foods, tools, armor, farms, travel, traps, mobs, and clutch methods.

1. State a clear judging rule.
2. Show 3-5 candidates quickly.
3. Give visual evidence for each.
4. Reveal a defensible but debatable winner.
5. Ask what was robbed or let comments choose a real follow-up test.

## SMP Betrayal, Trap, Or Revenge

Best when actual footage exists or a role-play story can be deliberately recorded.

1. Show the theft, attack, discovery, or trap payoff.
2. Explain the relationship and stakes in one sentence.
3. Build the counterplay.
4. Reveal the outcome and recover or lose something meaningful.
5. Resolve this episode, then tease a genuine consequence.

Never imply staged footage is an authentic betrayal. Do not invent a friend's future retaliation merely to force a sequel.

## Build Or Transformation

Best for before/after bases, biome changes, impossible houses, farms, and time-limited builds.

1. Flash the final transformation.
2. Show the ugly or empty starting point.
3. Use three major progress beats.
4. Reveal a functional or visual surprise.
5. Ask viewers to choose the next upgrade.

## Update Or Snapshot

Best immediately after an official Minecraft release, snapshot, preview, or event.

1. Show the most visible verified change.
2. Explain what changed and the applicable edition/version.
3. Demonstrate why it matters.
4. Show a benefit, risk, or limitation.
5. Ask what verified feature to test next.

Browse official Minecraft sources first. Do not manufacture patch urgency or say "before it gets patched" without evidence.

## Speedrun Or Item Challenge

Best for "obtain X," timed routes, limited inventory, wheel challenges, or escalating restrictions.

1. Show the target and timer/result.
2. State one simple rule.
3. Show two meaningful progress jumps.
4. Introduce a late obstacle.
5. Reveal the time or failure, then raise one variable for the next episode.

## Testing Subscriber Comments

Best for converting comments into a recurring channel identity.

1. Show the exact short comment on screen.
2. Translate it into a clear test.
3. Run the test with visible controls.
4. Resolve it honestly.
5. Invite one next comment category or 2-3 choices.
