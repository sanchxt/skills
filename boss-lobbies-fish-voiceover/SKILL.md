# Boss Lobbies TTS Voiceover

Create gameplay-first voiceovers with a clear player point of view: a live reaction, playful roast, cocky flex, shared frustration, or conspiratorial observation. The listener should hear a person who is entertained by what is unfolding, not a polished ad narrator describing it. Put the game, proof, payoff, and personality ahead of promotion. Treat any Boss Services mention as a quick, earned close - never as the premise of the video.

## Gather the brief

Confirm or infer from the supplied footage:

- Game, mode, platform, and the video's one promised outcome.
- Current-fact status: official source, independent test, or reported/unverified. Capture the version and test/source date when relevant.
- Footage/proof available and non-negotiable details.
- Target length. If none is supplied, choose the shortest band that shows the promise and proof.
- Whether the user explicitly wants a CTA, engagement prompt, or both. Do not infer that a service CTA is wanted merely because the content is for Boss Lobbies.
- The TTS engine if given. For ElevenLabs, retain expressive square-bracket performance cues when requested.

Browse official game sources first for time-sensitive claims. If a claim cannot be verified, call it reported and never guarantee it. Do not invent a patch, method, source, service, result, or platform support.

When the active workspace contains `BOSS_LOBBIES_RESEARCH.md` or `BOSS_LOBBIES_CONTENT_SYSTEM_AND_SOURCES.md`, read the relevant sections before writing. Follow their source hierarchy, glitch checklist, supported-service catalog, rights rules, and game-matched CTA rule.

Read [references/script-and-tts-patterns.md](references/script-and-tts-patterns.md) before selecting the hook, personality premise, or TTS cues.

## Build the narration

1. Choose one hook engine and make the first spoken line specific to the game. Start on the result, danger, surprising contrast, painful mistake, or a question the clip instantly answers.
2. Write like a confident player reacting to another player, not a guide, disclaimer, or ad. Give the draft one unmistakable comic premise grounded in the footage: for example, the enemies are donating kills, the lobby is an emotional-support lobby, or leaving would be irresponsible. Carry that point of view through the narration with two or three real reaction beats. Do not add slang or jokes that could fit any game.
3. Name the exact game/mode and promise immediately after the hook. Use only facts the footage can support.
4. Progress through two or three proof beats. Make every beat change the viewer's understanding, stakes, or visible result.
5. Land the payoff cleanly. For reported methods, use a short on-screen status label such as `REPORTED - VERIFY CURRENT BUILD` or a caption note by default. Speak the caveat only when it is the meaningful reveal; never lead with it or let it dominate the narration.
6. End with one action only: a follow/comment prompt, a gameplay punchline, or - only when requested - a game-matched Boss Services CTA. An engagement close can be direct and playful, such as asking viewers to comment a supplied keyword for the details; do not promise an unverified method or unsupported service.

### Entertainment and promotion guardrails

- Never open a gameplay Short with a source disclaimer, patch note, service, brand, or legal-sounding warning unless the post is specifically a patch/source-check video.
- Make gameplay the premise. Do not make a service the premise, reveal, or final thought unless the user explicitly asks for a conversion-focused post.
- Default to a no-service-CTA draft when the user does not request a CTA. Do not include an ad variant merely because a related service exists.
- For a glitch, farm, or indirect service fit, include a service variant only when the user explicitly requests one. Keep it separate from the gameplay-first draft.
- Reject and rewrite any script if its first two spoken lines could belong to an official help article, if the caveat is longer than the payoff, or if removing the brand would make the clip noticeably less fun.
- Reject and rewrite narration that merely lists gameplay events (for example, kills, XP, streaks) without a memorable attitude about them. The footage must be the subject of the joke or reaction.
- Prefer a concrete, quotable line over generic hype: `why would I ever leave this lobby?`, `the kill feed is printing receipts`, or `leaving would be irresponsible` are useful shapes when the footage supports them. Do not reuse these lines mechanically.
- When the user asks for experimentation or more personality, provide three to five complete drafts with different hook engines and comic premises. Do not merely swap adjectives or delivery cues.

Use these starting bands as production hypotheses, then improve them from retention data:

| Finished length | Spoken words | Use when |
|---|---:|---|
| 15-22 seconds | 45-65 | One visual payoff or one quick warning |
| 22-35 seconds | 70-100 | Two proof beats or a short route |
| 36-50 seconds | 105-135 | A real test, comparison, or three necessary beats |

Favor the shortest complete explanation. Compare retention among similar videos; do not claim that gamers universally prefer the shortest video.

## Format for Fish Audio S2.1 Pro and ElevenLabs

Return one clean `TTS copy/paste` block per draft, or name the requested engine (for example, `ElevenLabs copy/paste`). The block must contain only words and performance cues to synthesize; never include timestamps, camera directions, markdown, hashtags, or explanatory labels.

- Write short, spoken sentences. Use natural commas and periods; use an ellipsis only for deliberate suspense.
- Spell names, acronyms, numbers, dates, URLs, and unusual terms in their spoken form when it prevents a bad read. For example, write `Black Ops Seven`, not `BO7`, if that is how it should sound.
- When the user requests expressions or is using ElevenLabs, use two to four inline square-bracket performance cues per draft. Place a short, natural cue exactly where the delivery turns, such as `[excited, can't believe it]`, `[quick laugh]`, `[playfully cocky]`, `[low, conspiratorial]`, or `[direct, inviting]`. These cues should carry a change in attitude, not decorate every line.
- For Fish Audio, use zero to three S2-style square-bracket cues. For ElevenLabs, preserve the requested brackets and test a preview; simplify or remove a cue if the chosen voice reads it aloud.
- Let the selected voice supply its own identity. Do not prescribe accent, age, or gender unless the brief asks for it.

## Decide the Boss Services CTA

Decide a CTA only after the information-first script is complete. If the user did not ask for a CTA, deliver the no-service version only.

| Situation | Deliver |
|---|---|
| User explicitly requests a matching service CTA and an exact service fits the demonstrated grind or outcome | One integrated script with a brief final CTA naming the matching game/outcome. |
| User explicitly requests CTA options, but the fit is indirect, the clip is a glitch, or the brief is ambiguous | Two complete variants: `With service CTA` and `No service CTA`. Explain the uncertainty in one sentence outside the blocks. |
| User did not request a CTA, or no matching supported service exists | Deliver only the no-service version. Mention no verified fit only if it affects the requested output. |

Keep a requested CTA to the final two to three seconds and phrase it as a choice, not a promise. Do not claim a completion time, guarantee, safety, permanence, or outcome the catalog/source does not support.

## Deliver

Lead with the clean script or scripts. Then include only the production context needed to use them:

1. `TTS copy/paste` - one block per draft, labelled for Fish Audio or ElevenLabs when specified. When experimenting, label each draft with its distinct hook and comic premise outside the block.
2. `Runtime and word count` - estimate from actual spoken words.
3. `Hook` - name the hook engine and first-frame proof required.
4. `Claim status` - identify confirmed, independently tested, or reported status; include source/version/date if supplied or researched. State any required on-screen caveat here rather than making it an unnecessary spoken disclaimer.
5. `TTS preview note` - name the one emotional transition to listen for. Do not prescribe voice traits that were not supplied.
6. `CTA decision` - include this only when a CTA was requested or supplied.

Keep the spoken script conversational, playful, and direct. Avoid generic hype, fake urgency, long feature lists, unsupported "still works" claims, legalese, and hard-selling before the payoff.
