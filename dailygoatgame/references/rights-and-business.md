# Rights, originality, and business

This reference is an operating framework, not legal clearance. Verify current platform rules when a decision depends on them. The owner has deferred budget and operating country; don't block ordinary ideation on those details.

## Three separate questions

1. **Rights:** May this account use the source footage, streamer performance/audio, game material, and music in the intended way on both platforms?
2. **Originality and distribution:** Does the actual edit add meaningful new value, and is it eligible for recommendations?
3. **Monetization:** Does the channel and each relevant post meet the earning program's requirements?

Permission can help question 1 without resolving 2 or 3. No claim appearing at upload is not permission or guaranteed earning eligibility. A streamer's existing commentary isn't DailyGoatGame's original commentary.

Prefer owner-recorded material if available, permissioned submissions, clearly applicable creator licenses, or official assets within their usage terms. "Official" means authoritative for facts, not automatically reusable. Native remix/music permissions may be platform-specific and should not be assumed to carry into the exported cross-platform master.

Subtitles, crop, speed/pitch changes, watermark removal, or a logo do not establish rights. Nor do credit or disclaimers. There is no safe number of seconds or percentage of original overlays that guarantees fair use or platform approval. Don't give legal certainty based on a text-only edit plan.

## Originality without narration

Possible editorial contributions: annotate the cause of a surprising play; match evidence from two versions; explain what an update changes through an original visual demonstration; contextualize an authentic reaction with verified footage; or build a new evidence-based story from selected excerpts. These are creative routes to assess, not approval recipes.

If a requested edit is essentially someone else's moment plus subtitles, say that plainly and give one practical stronger variant. Do not derail every creative output with a policy lecture. Do not force ElevenLabs as a compliance shortcut. Voiceover is neither always necessary nor automatically sufficient.

Keep source, permission/license evidence, original script/cards, and project files. Review the entire channel's pattern, not a supposed safe proportion of original posts. Meaningless scrolling text and mass-produced news cards can create originality issues too.

## Monetization planning when asked

Treat ads, sponsorships, affiliate conversions, and fan support as separate revenue paths. Check current YPP tiers; early fan-funding access is not the same as Shorts ad sharing. Use valid engaged-view eligibility data from Studio rather than the public play counter. Don't promise approval at a view threshold.

Potential sponsor categories to research: games and legitimate publishers, PC/console peripherals, capture/audio equipment, and relevant gaming services. Match offers to the audience's actual interests. Do not write a positive review of a product the owner hasn't used or imply a streamer endorses a sponsor.

For affiliates verify program eligibility, allowed traffic, disclosure terms, territory, and product availability. Clearly disclose material relationships in the actual content where required, using current platform tools and applicable guidance. Do not invent an affiliate account, code, tracking link, conversion rate, or expected earnings.

A media kit should use the owner's verified audience geography, typical recent reach (not only a record viral post), shares/saves, prior campaign results if any, editorial fit, deliverables, and rights scope. Request operating country and commercial details only at the stage where they matter. Draft pitches; do not contact creators or sponsors without explicit authorization.
