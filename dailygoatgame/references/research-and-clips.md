# Research and clip discovery

## Scope and freshness

Record the research date and intended audience. For "today/recent/latest", start with the last 24–72 hours; widen explicitly to 7 days if needed. If that still fails, return fewer good leads or clearly labeled archival material. For upcoming releases, normally examine the next 30–90 days, adapting to the request. Research publication date, actual event date, original clip date, and verification date separately.

Do not use this skill's 2026 foundation as a current trend list. Browse each time claims depend on current events. Without browsing, offer evergreen formats and say current validation was unavailable.

## Source hierarchy

- Facts: publisher/studio newsrooms, dated patch notes, official trailers, developer statements, platform store pages, official event schedules.
- Creator behavior: original stream/VOD/clip on the creator's actual channel, including surrounding context. An official highlights upload may help, but distinguish it from the original event.
- Discovery: reputable gaming coverage, Reddit game communities, r/LivestreamFail, creator fan communities, YouTube/Instagram results and comments. Verify material claims upstream.
- Attention: Steam charts, Twitch categories/analytics, Google Trends by relevant country, dated social discussion, comparable video performance. Player counts, livestream hours, search interest, and Shorts demand measure different things.

Useful starting points: [Rockstar](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire), [Minecraft](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/articles), [Apex](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/apex-legends/news), [Fortnite](https://www.fortnite.com/news), [Steam charts](https://store.steampowered.com/charts/mostplayed), [Steam news](https://store.steampowered.com/news/), [Twitch categories](https://www.twitch.tv/directory), [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/), and each game's official site. Open and verify the relevant dated item rather than cite a homepage for a detailed claim.

Use Reddit to identify confusion, desires, repeated jokes, and disagreements. Upvotes are not permission or representative audience research. Search creators and games together; exclude unrelated IRL drama. A famous streamer is a source lead, not automatically a compelling moment. Include smaller creators when their payoff and source access are better.

## Evidence before ranking

For each serious candidate establish:

| Field | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Topic and angle | Specific cold-viewer promise, not just a game name |
| Why now | Actual dated trigger; classify evergreen if none |
| Factual source | Direct primary URL where available; label reporting-only support |
| Footage source | Creator/official source URL, distinct from a discovery article |
| Source dates | Original event, upload, and checked date; unknown is allowed |
| Viewing status | Video inspected / transcript reviewed / metadata only / discovery lead |
| Original contribution | What DailyGoatGame adds beyond extracting the moment |
| Audio mode | Original audio / text-led / hybrid / narration with reason |
| Payoff and effort | What viewers get; relative low/medium/high effort, not invented hours |
| Rights status | Owned / specific permission or license verified / unknown / restricted |
| Main goal | Share, save, discussion, follow, or repeat viewing |

An idea score is a comparative editorial judgment, never a predicted probability. Score clarity, payoff, audience fit, freshness, originality opportunity, and feasible assets from 0–2 each if ranking helps. Keep evidence/rights readiness separate: a high score cannot cancel an unverified claim or unusable asset. Show a short rationale instead of false numerical precision when evidence is thin.

"Trending" needs a dated trigger plus corroborating attention evidence. Otherwise say **current news opportunity**, **active player base**, or **discovery lead**. Do not claim velocity from a single total view count; timestamp two observations or label it unmeasured. Syndicated stories are not independent confirmation.

## Clip inspection

Find the original upload from links, attribution, recognizable audio, or creator pages. Check whether a resurfaced clip is old. Inspect enough before and after the moment to establish who speaks, what caused the reaction, the game/mode, and whether it was staged or modded. Avoid edited subtitles that contradict the audio.

Give precise **source** in/out timestamps only after video or time-coded transcript inspection. Say whether the timestamp is in the VOD or local clip. A transcript can verify words, but cannot verify a visual gag; full visual claims need visual inspection. A search thumbnail and title do not establish the full scene.

If direct viewing fails, return the exact discovery/source links you verified, the proposed angle, and the missing verification. Never invent timestamps to make a lead look complete. If the user asks for five recently posted reactions and only two are verified, deliver two verified and separate any promising leads. Do not silently broaden the date window or mislabel ten-year-old horror clips as current.

Never use an aggregator's finished edit as the presumed raw source. Inspect broadcaster clipping/reuse terms and media rights where available. The native ability to create a Twitch clip does not itself establish commercial cross-platform permission. Route uncertain reuse through the rights reference; offer a permissioned clip, original capture if available, or original information graphic as an alternative.

## Facts, leaks, and trailer analysis

Use a compact claim ledger for factual videos: claim, primary URL, date/version, confidence, exact permitted wording. Resolve old/new release-date conflicts in favor of the latest official statement; keep launch, preload, early access, and regional dates distinct.

For patch content distinguish live release, test server, Java snapshot, Bedrock Preview, mod, DLC, and rumor. Do not call a preview "Minecraft just added this for everyone." For dates include time zone when there is a time, and do not invent one when only a day is published.

For trailer details distinguish **shown**, **officially stated**, **plausible inference**, and **fan theory**. Don't call a camera cut a mistake, promise unannounced features, invent numbered secrets to fill a list, or imply official footage is a leak. Report a leak's existence and attribution if relevant; prefer public official material for the edit and avoid treating stolen builds as required assets.

## Competitor research

Read the foundation for seed accounts. For a fresh audit, inspect accessible recent and popular content, not just all-time winners. When practical use 6–10 recent posts plus 3–5 high performers for each selected account, and record what was actually available. Compare same-platform, similar-age, similar-length posts. Record titles, dates, duration, view count capture time, first-frame premise, context, payoff, CTA, and content source. Only describe visual/audio editing you inspected.

Follower totals don't reveal follow conversion; public views don't prove profit. Don't infer monetization from ads, third-party earnings calculators, or "is monetized" badges. Separate direct comparables from adjacent inspiration and large publisher resources from a solo workflow. Learn their formats and rights pipelines; write distinct ideas and copy.
