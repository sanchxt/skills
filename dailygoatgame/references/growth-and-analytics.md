# Growth, calendars, and analytics

## Launch test

Treat the first month as learning about repeatable demand. The account can cover many games if viewers recognize a common reason to watch. Use a few related clusters initially to make that hypothesis testable. Do not insist that every new game "confuses the algorithm."

Suggested 60-post base month, two originals/day cross-posted: 24 funny/reaction moments, 18 useful visual updates, 12 details/comparisons, 6 experiments. Third posts are optional strong opportunities. This is a production allocation, not a research-proven optimum.

First 14 days: roughly 12 moments, 8 news, 6 details, 2 experiments across 28 base posts. Use 2–4 repeatable formats rather than 28 unrelated concepts. Slot in verified breaking news when it matters; keep evergreen material for quiet days. Recheck dates before scheduled news publishes.

After the initial sample, favor the best two or three series, retain some exploration, and keep testing. Don't lock the account forever because one celebrity clip spikes.

## Cadence and timing

Two strong daily posts plus a genuinely useful third suits the owner's capacity. YouTube does not require a minimum posting cadence for distribution. Don't claim more posts inherently reduce reach or that a new account needs artificial "warming up."

Use stable, sustainable posting windows for comparison. Timely news should not wait for a magic hour. If the owner requests a schedule, label proposed windows as tests in the target audience's time zone, convert with the actual date and daylight-saving rules, and do not infer the operating country from the machine. Replace hypotheses with viewer-activity data when available.

## Collect comparable observations

Track concept ID, platform, series, game, source/event date, publication date/time zone, duration, audio mode, hook, CTA, original contribution, asset/rights status, edit effort, and performance capture time. Each platform gets its own row linked by concept ID.

Use 24h and 7d observations as convenient working checkpoints, not statements that distribution is finished. Compare posts at the same age. Exclude paid boosts from organic comparisons or label them separately. Preserve a monthly or 28-day view of returning audiences.

| YouTube | Instagram |
|---|---|
| Engaged views; chose-to-view/stayed-to-watch; average duration/percentage viewed; retention where available; subscribers gained; returning viewers at channel level | Accounts reached; average watch time/retention where available; shares; saves; likes; comments; follows attributed to the Reel; profile activity where available |

Availability and names change; use actual exported labels. Keep public views and engaged views separate. Neither platform's view count is interchangeable with the other's. If only public numbers are supplied, do not invent retention, sends, saves, conversion, demographics, or eligibility.

Useful descriptive ratios:

- YouTube subscribers gained per 1,000 engaged views.
- Instagram follows per 1,000 accounts reached, where attributed follows exist.
- Instagram shares and saves per 1,000 reached, kept separate. Public shares are not necessarily isolated DM sends.
- Total watch time divided by production effort when reliable inputs exist, as an efficiency indicator.

State denominator, window, and raw numerator. Missing/zero denominators produce N/A, not zero or infinity. A handful of follows on tiny reach is noisy. Use within-format medians and distributions; don't let one outlier set the entire benchmark. Rewatch can push percentage viewed above 100%; it does not establish satisfaction by itself.

## Diagnose before prescribing

| Pattern | Plausible explanation to investigate | Next test |
|---|---|---|
| Many swipe away, reasonable retention among those who stay | Opening/context mismatch | Clearer first frame and shorter setup on a new comparable concept |
| Strong opening, drop before payoff | Delay, confusing crop, text overload, misleading promise | Move proof earlier; reduce explanation; check original sequence |
| Many views, few follows | One-off celebrity interest; weak recurring promise | Adjacent episode with recognizable benefit, profile/series clarity |
| Saves but weak reach | Useful content with insufficient immediate relevance | Lead with player consequence rather than patch/version label |
| Shares, low completion | Humor may be clear early or ending drags | Retain the useful reaction; test a tighter aftermath |
| Good ratios, low distribution | Small sample, topic demand, competition, eligibility | Check status and compare later; don't assume a shadowban |
| One platform wins repeatedly | Packaging/audience differences | Preserve core edit but test native title/cover/caption/audio choices |

These are hypotheses, not diagnoses proved by one metric. Inspect the video and competing explanations. Never promise one edit will restore reach.

## Decisions

Try several comparable examples (often 6–10 where practical) before abandoning a format. This is a planning heuristic, not statistical significance. Change one main controllable factor per test; topic, creator, timing, and luck still confound comparisons. Organic posts are not randomized A/B experiments.

Scale a series when retention plus the intended action repeatedly outperform its comparable baseline and sourcing remains sustainable. Rework high-view, low-conversion formats before expanding them. Retire weak, costly, or unoriginal formats after repeated evidence; don't delete/reupload flops automatically.

Give each review: strongest finding, uncertainty, what to keep, what to change, the next three concrete tests, and what evidence would reverse the recommendation. Financial goals require actual business data; don't extrapolate RPM from strangers' screenshots.
